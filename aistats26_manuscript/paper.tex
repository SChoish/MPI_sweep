\begin{abstract}
TD3+BC uses the same actor branch for bootstrapped critic targets and deployment, coupling bootstrap exposure to policy reach. We introduce budgeted actor refinement (BAR), a persistent $K$-actor chain whose first actor supplies TD-target actions and whose final actor is deployed. A finite-step bound makes target perturbation depend on first-hop displacement, while an ideal Wasserstein--2 proximal analysis describes the complementary local geometry. On nine D4RL locomotion tasks, fourteen budgets, and four proximal seeds, the equally weighted $T\ge4$ mean is strictly ordered by depth, rising from $25.38$ at $K=1$ to $63.97$ at $K=4$ as score-$<20$ cells fall from $34/63$ to $8/63$; both host-separated seed pairs reproduce the full ordering. A four-seed projected-linearized sweep also gains $8.01$ points from $K=2$ to $K=3$, with a 95\% task-resampling interval $[3.56,13.53]$. The proximal $K=4$--$K=3$ gain is $12.06$ with a 95\% interval $[3.84,21.11]$, but its $.34$ cellwise median and eight rescues show that lower-tail recovery drives the aggregate. A matched 270-checkpoint TD3/P2/P3 audit finds that BAR-Prox $K=3$ lowers next-state target displacement relative to TD3+BC in $88/90$ cells while typical final-policy displacement does not contract; a separate four-seed $K=4$ audit lowers target displacement in $89/90$ matched seed-0/1 comparisons with TD3+BC. Thus depth is a finite-step stability control: moderate refinement decouples exposure from reach, while added depth further suppresses first-hop exposure and rescues failures.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:introduction}

Offline reinforcement learning (RL) seeks a policy from a fixed dataset without additional environment interaction. Its central difficulty is not merely to select a high-value action, but to determine how far policy improvement can proceed before the learned critic becomes unreliable. TD3+BC regularizes a deterministic actor toward dataset actions while using a Polyak copy of that actor in bootstrapped critic targets \citep{fujimoto2021minimalist}. The same policy branch therefore determines both the action shift entering critic training and the reach of the evaluated policy. Aggressive improvement can move target actions into regions where critic error is amplified, whereas conservative improvement can leave useful policy improvement unrealized.

We study this coupling through \emph{budgeted actor refinement} (BAR), a re-centered actor architecture for TD3+BC. BAR represents a nominal improvement budget with $K$ sequential actor updates. The first actor is anchored to dataset actions, and each later actor is anchored to the stop-gradient output of its predecessor. A target copy of the first refined actor supplies the next actions in TD targets, while the final actor is used for evaluation. The architecture thus assigns critic-target construction and deployment to distinct policy branches without changing the critic update rule.

Let $D_{\mathrm{critic}}$ denote the squared displacement of the critic-coupled first-hop branch and let $D_{\mathrm{final}}$ denote that of the evaluated final policy. A one-hop actor identifies these quantities; BAR assigns them to $\mu_1$ and $\mu_K$, respectively. This separation can increase final-policy reach at comparable first-hop movement or contract both movements near an unstable training outcome. It also introduces a countervailing mismatch because the critic target follows the $\mu_1$ branch while evaluation follows $\mu_K$. The method therefore exposes a design axis between target-policy displacement and deployment reach rather than guaranteeing that either quantity is independently optimal.

For deterministic actors, the squared TD3+BC anchor is a Wasserstein--2
movement cost in the Dirac-policy limit. With a fixed critic and small realized
steps, ideal explicit and implicit updates discretize the same flow to first
order. The large-budget tail instead probes the coupled finite-step regime,
where projection, actor fitting, critic drift, saturation, and optimization
history determine the end-to-end outcome.

The complete proximal phase diagram yields high-budget means $25.38\to41.20\to51.92\to63.97$ for $K=1,2,3,4$ and low-return counts $34\to22\to16\to8$. Both disjoint seed pairs reproduce the ordering; eight rescues account for $54.7\%$ of the $K=3\!\to\!4$ gain. A four-seed projected-linearized sweep reproduces the $K=2\!\to\!3$ direction. Direct diagnostics separate a moderate-depth regime, where target exposure contracts without typical final-policy contraction, from added-depth first-hop contraction. Failed runs also exhibit extreme TD-error tails, larger movement, and gain reversal under a common critic.

Our contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate BAR for TD3+BC as a re-centered actor composition that assigns target-policy displacement and final-policy reach to distinct branches.
    \item We give a Wasserstein proximal interpretation, derive an inexact finite-step margin with an explicit critic-error term, bound first-hop TD-target perturbation, and separate these statements from the fixed-critic small-step flow limit.
    \item Four-seed proximal sweeps through $K=4$, a four-seed linearized sweep through $K=3$, direct target-action measurements, and targeted controls show that depth broadens the large-budget stability envelope; the largest proximal gain is driven primarily by lower-tail rescue.
\end{itemize}

\section{RELATED WORK}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Behavior-regularized offline actor--critic learning.}
Offline actor--critic methods must control value extrapolation when a learned
policy selects actions outside the fixed dataset. Prior work studies this
failure through constrained policy improvement, conservative value learning,
and restrictions on actions used in Bellman backups
\citep{fujimoto2019offpolicy,kumar2019bear}. TD3+BC provides a particularly
minimal control: a deterministic actor maximizes a normalized critic while a
squared cloning term anchors its actions to the dataset
\citep{fujimoto2021minimalist}. We keep this critic update and actor family
fixed and study which refined actor branch supplies the TD target.
One-step offline RL instead limits repeated policy optimization against an
off-policy value estimate \citep{brandfonbrener2021offline}. BAR retains
iterative actor--critic training but distributes a nominal improvement
coefficient across a routed actor chain.

\paragraph{Policy geometry and proximal refinement.}
Trust-region policy optimization is commonly derived from information geometry
\citep{amari2000methods,schulman2015trust}. Optimal-transport geometry gives a
different notion of movement: policy optimization can be expressed as a
Wasserstein gradient flow, with related natural-gradient and trust-region
constructions for continuous control
\citep{zhang2018policy,moskovitz2020efficient,terpin2022trust,song2023metric}.
Wasserstein Policy Optimization projects Wasserstein policy flow into a
parametric actor class using value-function action gradients
\citep{pfau2025wpo}.
For deterministic policies, the statewise Wasserstein--2 cost reduces to
squared action displacement. We use that identity to interpret the TD3+BC
anchor as a proximal movement cost, while separating the finite-step
variational statement from its small-step flow limit.

\paragraph{Target networks and actor--critic coupling.}
Bootstrapping error can feed critic extrapolation back into actor updates
\citep{fujimoto2019offpolicy,kumar2019bear}. Polyak averaging delays this
feedback but ordinarily copies the same actor branch that will be deployed.
MCEP instead pairs a conservative target policy for value learning with a
mildly constrained evaluation policy excluded from Bellman backups
\citep{xu2024mcep}. BAR shares this routing principle but uses a persistent,
predecessor-recentered chain and varies depth under a fixed nominal budget;
MCEP keeps both actors directly data-anchored with separately chosen strengths
and is not a $K=2$ instance of BAR. Our contribution concerns this depth-indexed
routing operator rather than a new critic objective.

\section{PRELIMINARIES}
\label{sec:background}

We consider a discounted Markov decision process
$\langle\mathcal S,\mathcal A,P,r,\gamma\rangle$ with continuous actions and
a fixed offline dataset $\mathcal D=\{(s_i,a_i,r_i,s_i')\}_{i=1}^N$. A clipped
double critic is trained from targets
\begin{equation}
y_{\rho}=r+\gamma\min_j Q_j^-(s',\rho(s')),
\label{eq:td_target_background}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is a target policy. When $\rho(s')$ leaves dataset support,
critic error enters the regression target and can be amplified by later actor
updates \citep{fujimoto2019offpolicy,kumar2019bear}.

For a deterministic actor $\mu_\theta$, normalized TD3+BC minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\rm actor}(\theta)
=-\alpha\,\mathbb E_s\bar Q(s,\mu_\theta(s))
+\mathbb E_{(s,a)\sim\mathcal D}\frac{\|\mu_\theta(s)-a\|_2^2}{d},
\label{eq:td3bc_background}
\end{equation}
where $\bar Q=\widehat Q/C$ and $C>0$ is a stop-gradient critic scale
\citep{fujimoto2021minimalist}. Ordinarily, a Polyak copy of this actor enters
Eq.~\eqref{eq:td_target_background}, while the online actor is deployed.
Target-policy movement and deployment reach therefore follow the same branch.

A metric proximal update with step $h>0$ takes the form
\begin{equation}
\pi^+\in\argmin_\pi\left\{\widehat{\mathcal E}(\pi)
+\frac{1}{2h}d_{\mathcal M}^2(\pi,\pi^{\rm ref})\right\}.
\label{eq:generic_prox}
\end{equation}
This minimizing-movement construction is associated with metric gradient
flows \citep{ambrosio2005gradient,otto2001geometry}. For deterministic
state-conditioned policies,
\begin{equation}
W_2^2(\delta_{\mu(s)},\delta_{\nu(s)})=\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2.
\label{eq:dirac_w2_background}
\end{equation}
Thus the TD3+BC anchor is a Wasserstein--2 movement cost up to fixed scaling.
We distinguish its finite-step variational meaning from the small-step,
fixed-critic gradient-flow limit and from large-budget coupled training.

\section{BUDGETED ACTOR REFINEMENT}
\label{sec:method}

At each delayed actor update, BAR maintains $K$ independently initialized,
persistent deterministic actors with separate optimizer states. The first
actor takes one TD3+BC update toward the sampled dataset actions; each later
actor takes one update toward the stop-gradient output of its predecessor on
the same minibatch. The online critic is fixed within this chain; we denote its first Q-function by
$\widehat Q$. A Polyak copy of the first actor supplies TD-target
actions, and the final actor is evaluated. We first derive the proximal
operator underlying these updates and then formalize their composition.
\subsection{Offline actor updates as proximal improvement}

Let $\rho_{\mathcal D}$ be the empirical state distribution and consider a
deterministic actor $\mu:\mathcal S\rightarrow\mathcal A\subset\mathbb R^d$.
At one training iteration, the critic parameters are held fixed while the actor
chain is updated. We write the current critic as $\widehat Q_t$, use
$\epsilon=10^{-6}$, and define the implementation-level proximal scales by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 C_{1,t}^{\rm P}
 &=\sg\!\left(\mathbb E_s
 |\widehat Q_t(s,\mu_{1,t}^{\rm pre}(s))|+\epsilon\right),\\
 C_{k,t}^{\rm P}
 &=\sg\!\left(\mathbb E_s
 |\widehat Q_t(s,\sg\mu_{k-1,t}^{+}(s))|+\epsilon\right),\quad k\ge2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:qnorm}
\end{equation}
The normalized critic is
$\bar Q_{k,t}(s,a)=\widehat Q_t(s,a)/C_{k,t}^{\rm P}$.
Here \emph{pre} and the superscript $+$ denote actions before and after the
current actor update, and $\sg$ denotes stop-gradient. We suppress $t$ and the
superscript when discussing the ideal operator. A fixed positive $C$ only
rescales gradient-flow time; recomputing $C_k$ changes the finite-step schedule.
Our finite-step statements condition on the realized scales.

For deterministic policies and a state distribution $\rho$, define the
action-averaged Wasserstein metric
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf d_{d,\rho}^2(\mu,\nu)
 :=\mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}
 \left[\frac{1}{d}\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2\right].
 \label{eq:mean_metric}
\end{equation}
It is $d^{-1}$ times the state-averaged squared $W_2$ distance between
state-conditioned Dirac policies. We abbreviate
$\mathsf d_d=\mathsf d_{d,\rho_{\mathcal D}}$ on the actor-training state
distribution.
The population counterpart of squared behavior cloning also admits a
deterministic reference. Let $P_{\!B}$ be the data-generating behavior
distribution, let $\rho_B$ be its state marginal, and define
$b(s)=\mathbb E_{P_{\!B}}[a\mid s]$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E_{(s,a)\sim P_{\!B}}\frac{1}{d}\|\mu(s)-a\|_2^2
 =\mathsf d_{d,\rho_B}^2(\mu,b)+\mathrm{const},
 \label{eq:bc_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where the constant is the conditional action variance and does not depend on
$\mu$. The empirical actor loss is a Monte Carlo estimate of the left-hand
side, so samplewise squared anchoring targets the conditional-mean behavior map
at the population level.

Given a reference policy $\nu$, step $h>0$, and fixed normalized critic
$\bar Q$, define the proximal refinement operator by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P_{h}^{\bar Q}(\nu)
\in\argmin_{\mu\in\mathcal M}
\left\{-\mathbb E_s\bar Q(s,\mu(s))
+\frac{1}{2h}\mathsf d_d^2(\mu,\nu)\right\}.
\label{eq:prox_operator}
\end{equation}
At hop $k$, setting $\bar Q=\bar Q_k$ and multiplying by $2h$ gives the
practical actor objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{h,k}(\mu\mid\nu)
=-2h\,\mathbb E_s\bar Q_k(s,\mu(s))
+\mathsf d_d^2(\mu,\nu).
\label{eq:actorloss}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Behavior anchoring is Wasserstein proximal improvement]
\label{prop:prox_equiv}
For deterministic policies, the normalized anchored objective
\begin{equation*}
 -\alpha\,\mathbb E_s\bar Q(s,\mu(s))
 +\mathsf d_d^2(\mu,\nu)
\end{equation*}
is Eq.~\eqref{eq:prox_operator}, up to multiplication by a positive constant, with
$h=\alpha/2$. Equivalently, the raw-critic objective
$-\lambda\mathbb E_s\widehat Q(s,\mu(s))+\mathsf d_d^2(\mu,\nu)$ has
$h=\lambda C/2$ when $C$ is fixed. The population counterpart of squared
dataset anchoring uses $\nu=b$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:bc_decomposition}.
\end{proposition}

The proposition identifies an update operator relative to the \emph{estimated}
critic; true-return improvement lies outside the scope of this certificate.

\subsection{Re-centered composition and actor--critic dataflow}

For population analysis, let $\mu_0:=b$ denote the conditional-mean behavior
map and let $h_{1:K}$ be positive step sizes. The idealized BAR chain is
\begin{equation}
 \mu_k\in\mathcal P_{h_k}^{\bar Q_k}
 (\operatorname{sg}\mu_{k-1}),
 \qquad k=1,\ldots,K.
 \label{eq:bar}
\end{equation}
The implementation approximates each subproblem with one Adam update.
Its first actor $\mu_1$ uses sampled dataset actions as its reference; later
actors $\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_K$ use the preceding online actor under the same
current critic. Only the Polyak copy of $\mu_1$ enters the TD target, while
$\mu_K$ supplies the evaluated action. Because the later persistent actors are
independently initialized, their anchoring gradient need not vanish even at
$h=0$ when predecessor and current actor differ. The ideal proximal map
therefore motivates, but does not certify, the live one-Adam-step chain.

Our fixed-budget schedule uses the implementation parameter
$T=\tau=\alpha/2$, sets $h_k=T/K$, and holds $T$ fixed. Thus $K=1$ recovers
the TD3+BC coefficient $\alpha=2T$; $T$ is neither a compute budget nor a
realized distance constraint. Increasing $K$ jointly changes local coefficient,
re-centering frequency, actor count and compute, normalization trajectory, and
target/deployment dataflow. Each $K$ is therefore a complete randomized
procedure, and its seed average estimates marginal end-to-end performance.
Identical critic initialization across depths is a common-random-number
variance-reduction design, not a validity condition.

On the next-state distribution $\rho_{\mathcal D}'$ used by the TD target,
define the action-averaged population displacements
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
D_{\rm critic}&:=\mathsf d_{d,\rho_{\mathcal D}'}^2(\mu_1,\mu_0),\\
D_{\rm final}&:=\mathsf d_{d,\rho_{\mathcal D}'}^2(\mu_K,\mu_0).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:displacements}
\end{equation}
A one-step actor has $D_{\rm critic}=D_{\rm final}$; BAR assigns the two
population quantities to distinct policy branches. On fixed validation batches, we measure the sample-anchored online branches
and the deterministic next-state Polyak target branch:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat D_{\rm critic}&=\mathbb E_i\|\mu_1(s_i)-a_i\|_2^2/d,\\
\widehat D_{\rm final}&=\mathbb E_i\|\mu_K(s_i)-a_i\|_2^2/d,\\
\widehat D_{\rm target}&=\mathbb E_i\|\mu_1^-(s_i')-a_{i+1}\|_2^2/d.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:direct_target_displacement}
\end{equation}
The direct audit retains only bootstrapped transition pairs; a smoothed version
uses common TD3 target-noise draws. Sample anchoring adds a common
conditional-action-variance floor in population differences. These are
movement measurements, not critic-reliability estimates.

\subsection{Finite-step statements}

The following statements do not require a small-step Taylor expansion.

\begin{proposition}[Inexact estimated-energy margin]
\label{prop:margin}
Suppose $\mu_{k-1}$ is feasible for the $k$th actor class and the computed
actor satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal L_{h_k,k}(\mu_k\mid\mu_{k-1})
\leq
\mathcal L_{h_k,k}(\mu_{k-1}\mid\mu_{k-1})+\varepsilon_k.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 m_k^{\widehat Q}:={}&
 \frac{2h_k}{C_k}\mathbb E_s
 [\widehat Q(s,\mu_k(s))-\widehat Q(s,\mu_{k-1}(s))]\\
 &-\mathsf d_d^2(\mu_k,\mu_{k-1})
 \geq-\varepsilon_k.
\end{split}
\label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

For the first sample-anchored hop, a statewise dataset action is not generally
a feasible deterministic-policy comparator; the population reference in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bc_decomposition} or a behavior actor is required for a literal
certificate. For re-centered hops, the preceding actor is feasible by
construction.

The margin is relative to $\widehat Q$. Let a trusted objective be
$Q^\dagger$ and write $\widehat Q=Q^\dagger+e$. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 m_k^{Q^\dagger}=m_k^{\widehat Q}
 -\frac{2h_k}{C_k}\mathbb E_s[&e(s,\mu_k(s))\\
 &-e(s,\mu_{k-1}(s))].
\end{split}
\label{eq:critic_error_margin}
\end{equation}
Thus estimated-energy descent transfers to $Q^\dagger$ only when critic error
is controlled along the realized path. Equation~\eqref{eq:margin} certifies
the learned objective rather than monotone true return.

\begin{proposition}[First-hop TD-target perturbation bound]
\label{prop:exposure}
For clarity, consider a noiseless TD target using $\mu_1$,
$y_{\mu_1}=r+\gamma\min_jQ_j^-(s',\mu_1(s'))$, and suppose each target critic
$Q_j^-(s',\cdot)$ is $L(s')$-Lipschitz. For any reference $\mu_0$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\|y_{\mu_1}-y_{\mu_0}\|_{L_2(\mathcal D)}
\leq\gamma\Big(&\mathbb E_{s'}L(s')^2\\
&\cdot\|\mu_1(s')-\mu_0(s')\|_2^2\Big)^{1/2}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:exposure_bound}
\end{equation}
If $L(s')\leq\bar L$, the right-hand side is at most
$\gamma\bar L\sqrt{dD_{\rm critic}}$. Conditional on $\mu_1$, later actors do
not enter this one-step target perturbation.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure} bounds a one-step value-target difference, not
the stability of the learned critic. Its architectural content is that later
refinement can alter $D_{\rm final}$ without directly entering this bound.
With a Polyak target network, $\mu_1$ is replaced by its target copy. A
same-noise comparison under TD3 target smoothing obeys the same bound because
action clipping is nonexpansive. The current-state
$\widehat D_{\rm critic}$ omits Polyak lag and target noise, whereas
$\widehat D_{\rm target}$ measures the Polyak actor directly and has a
smoothed counterpart. Neither displacement measures the action Lipschitz
factor or the resulting change in critic value.

The separation introduces a complementary evaluation-policy mismatch: critic
targets follow the $\mu_1$ branch, whereas evaluation follows $\mu_K$.
Increasing $D_{\rm final}$ at fixed $D_{\rm critic}$ can therefore make the
critic less representative of the evaluated policy. We interpret BAR as
exposing a measurable target/deployment trade-off; its empirical return
captures the net effect of target movement, evaluation mismatch, actor compute,
and the coupled optimization trajectory.

\subsection{Local Wasserstein-flow limit}
\label{sec:local_limit}

The gradient-flow interpretation is a local organizing principle. Fix a critic
and an interior action $a_0$. Let $g=\nabla_a\widehat Q(a_0)$,
$H=\nabla_a^2\widehat Q(a_0)$, and $\beta=dh/C$. Before projection, the
action-metric-matched linearized and exact statewise proximal updates satisfy
\begin{equation}
\Delta a_{\rm lin}=\beta g,
\qquad
\Delta a_{\rm prox}=\beta\nabla_a\widehat Q(a_0+\Delta a_{\rm prox}).
\label{eq:local_steps}
\end{equation}
If the Hessian is locally Lipschitz and $\beta\|H\|<1$, the latter yields
\begin{equation}
\Delta a_{\rm prox}=\beta g+\beta^2Hg+O(\beta^3),
\label{eq:resolvent}
\end{equation}
and both ideal updates are first-order discretizations of
$\dot a=d\nabla_a\bar Q(a)$. With fixed $C$ this is a constant
time-rescaling of raw-critic gradient ascent; with a positive policy-dependent
global $C$ it follows the same direction under an adaptive time
parameterization. With only $C^2$ regularity, the safe remainder is $o(\beta^2)$. The
displayed expansion additionally requires small realized displacement, inactive
projection, and an exact statewise solve. At large budgets, our experiments
diagnose the resulting nonlocal
actor--critic regime instead of extrapolating Eq.~\eqref{eq:resolvent}. The
end-to-end return sweep, even at small nominal $T$, is not a frozen-critic
action-level consistency test of this limit.

\begin{proposition}[Local fixed-budget composition]
\label{prop:local_composition}
Let a smooth one-step update in local coordinates satisfy
$\Psi_h(x)=x+h f(x)+h^2p(x)+O(h^3)$. For fixed $K$ and $T\rightarrow0$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Psi_{T/K}^{K}(x_0)
={}&x_0+Tf_0\\
&+T^2\!\left[\frac{K-1}{2K}J_0f_0+\frac{1}{K}p_0\right]
+O(T^3).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:k_comp}
\end{equation}
where $f_0=f(x_0)$ and $J_0=Df(x_0)$. Coordinate explicit Euler has
$p_0=0$, whereas exact backward Euler has $p_0=J_0f_0$. Hence both are
first-order methods; for each fixed $K$, the $T\to0$ expansion has a leading
flow-bias coefficient proportional to $1/K$.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:local_composition} explains a possible fixed-critic
small-step benefit of substepping. It neither predicts the sign of true-return
change nor applies to the projected, saturated, moving-critic branches studied
at large $T$. The expansion is not a uniform $K\to\infty$ result.

\section{EXPERIMENTS}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate BAR on the nine D4RL locomotion tasks formed by HalfCheetah,
Hopper, and Walker2d with medium, medium-replay, and expert datasets
\citep{fu2020d4rl}. These tasks directly exercise deterministic continuous
control and provide distinct behavior-data regimes for a target-routing stress
test. Base implementations follow CORL configurations
\citep{tarasov2022corl}; scores are D4RL normalized returns. Additional
per-task results and diagnostic definitions are in
the Supplementary Material.

\paragraph{Fixed-budget protocol and estimand.}
We hold a total nominal budget $T$ fixed, split it across $K$ actor hops as
$h=T/K$, and sweep
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
T\in\{&0.05,0.1,0.2,0.4,0.7,1.5,2.5,\\
      &4,7,10,12,14,17,20\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
over all nine tasks. Both grids use seeds 0--3, for
$9\times14\times4=504$ runs per depth.
Proximal seeds 0--1 and 2--3 were executed on separate hosts and form disjoint
replication pairs. The grid is a high-resolution stability phase diagram rather
than a final benchmark alone. For $K=1$, Proposition~\ref{prop:prox_equiv}
maps $T$ to the normalized TD3+BC coefficient $\alpha=2T$.

We compare TD3+BC ($K=1$), proximal BAR with $K\in\{2,3,4\}$ (BAR-Prox), and an
action-metric-matched projected linearized target with $K\in\{2,3\}$ (BAR-Lin):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 a_k^{\rm tar}
 &=\Pi_{\mathcal A}\!\left[
 \nu_k+d\frac{T}{K}\frac{\nabla_a\widehat Q(s,\nu_k)}{C_{k,t}^{\rm L}}\right],\\
 \nu_1&=a,\qquad
 \nu_k=\sg\mu_{k-1,t}^{+}(s)\quad(k\ge2).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:linearized_target}
\end{equation}
At each hop, BAR-Lin detaches the reference, recomputes the $\widehat Q$
gradient and scale, clips the target to the action box, and takes one
mean-squared-error optimizer step with
the corresponding persistent actor. Its first-hop scale is
\begin{equation*}
C_{1,t}^{\rm L}=\sg\!\left(
\mathbb E_{(s,a)\sim\mathcal D}|\widehat Q_t(s,a)|+\epsilon\right),
\end{equation*}
whereas $C_{k,t}^{\rm L}=C_{k,t}^{\rm P}$ for $k\ge2$. Thus the factor $d$
matches the action metric, but the first-hop normalization point differs from
BAR-Prox; the comparison does not match the complete scale schedule. It uses the same
first-hop target and final-hop evaluation routing. All conditions match
datasets, critic updates, and the $10^6$-update horizon; every headline score is
the ten-episode D4RL normalized mean at the final checkpoint. Intermediate
evaluation cadence varies by run family and does not enter this estimand. A
$K$-hop condition performs $K$ actor optimizer steps per delayed update, so
depth comparisons are between complete training procedures. The end-to-end
intervention changes local coefficient, re-centering frequency, actor compute,
normalization trajectory, routing, and optimization history.

We test whether this intervention reduces large-budget low-return outcomes,
persists under the linearized realization, separates first-hop exposure from
final-policy reach, and exposes critic-validity signatures of failure.

\subsection{Additional actors broaden the stability envelope}

Figure~\ref{fig:stability} and Table~\ref{tab:stability} summarize the sweep. The high-budget estimand averages seeds and the seven $T\ge4$ budgets within each task, then weights tasks equally. A seed-mean environment--budget cell is descriptively collapsed below score $20$. Across proximal $K=1,2,3,4$, the mean rises $25.38\to41.20\to51.92\to63.97$ while collapsed cells fall $34\to22\to16\to8$ of 63. $K=4$ gains $12.06$ over $K=3$ with task-resampling interval $[3.84,21.11]$ and seven of nine positive task means, yet its cellwise median is only $+.34$: eight rescues, no reversals, contribute $54.7\%$ of the gain. The $T\ge10$ tail remains strictly ordered, $20.27\to31.01\to42.23\to56.79$. BAR-Lin $K=3$ raises the four-seed high-budget mean from $38.43$ to $46.44$ and reduces collapsed cells from 25 to 21; its $+1.37$ cellwise median indicates a broader shift. The largest proximal gain therefore acts chiefly through lower-tail rescue rather than a uniform shift.

\paragraph{Task dependence and replication.}
Responses range from smooth to sharply nonmonotone, so there is no universal
critic-valid radius. Nominal $T$ is the strongest absolute within-environment
rank predictor of return in eight of nine environments; realized movement is
a mechanism readout, not a substitute for the intervention. P4--P3 is positive
within all three dynamics families ($+.79,+13.04,+22.33$), and every
leave-one-family-out mean remains positive ($+6.92$ to $+17.69$). Both
host-separated seed pairs reproduce the high-budget ordering:
$25.97,42.02,52.78,63.61$ and $24.79,40.38,51.05,64.33$. These are disjoint-seed
replications of one implementation.

The linearized sweep shows that the gain is not unique to the proximal loss.
At high budgets, BAR-Lin with $K=3$ gains $8.01$ points over $K=2$
(95\% task-resampling interval $[3.56,13.53]$; eight of nine positive task means)
and reduces collapse cells from $25$ to $21$. Both disjoint seed pairs reproduce
the direction, with gains of $7.23$ and $8.80$. BAR-Prox remains stronger overall, so these
are comparisons of complete training procedures rather than isolated
integrator effects.

\subsection{Large-budget behavior is nonlocal and critic dependent}


At $T=20$, the projected BAR-Lin $K=2$ target is clipped at an action bound in
$94$--$95\%$ of states, and the effective action-step coefficient
$h_{\rm eff}$ is only $14\%$ and $9\%$ of the nominal $dT/K$ coefficient in
the two hops. These are medians over 18 environment--seed runs per hop. The
practical operation is projected target generation followed by amortized neural
regression under a moving critic. The sweep measures the resulting end-to-end
stability envelope; local truncation error lies outside this estimand.

Figure~\ref{fig:failure} shows the common failure signature. Environment
return supplies the external label: collapsed runs score below $20$. These
runs have orders-of-magnitude larger TD-error tails, larger critic magnitudes,
and larger realized movements. The controlled common critic is the frozen
final TD3+BC $T=1$ critic matched by environment and seed. A run-total gain
sums the mean $Q$ change across actor hops on the shared state batch. For
collapsed BAR-Lin runs, its median under the jointly trained critic is
$3.33\times10^{11}$; under the common critic it is $-1.42$, compared with
$+2.11$ for stable runs. For BAR-Prox, the common-critic gain changes from
$+2.51$ in stable runs to $-1.06$ in collapsed runs. This sign reversal
diagnoses cross-critic disagreement consistent with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:critic_error_margin}. A separate four-environment seed-2/3
sensitivity audit,
using a fixed TD3+BC $T=.05$ reference critic, preserves the split: all 45
stable gains are positive and all 35 collapsed gains are negative (Supplement).

A simulator-reference audit provides targeted evidence of critic miscalibration in the screened checkpoints: the
median signed error $\widehat Q_1(s,a_K)-Q_{\rm MC}^{\rho_1}(s,a_K)$ is
$+1.70\times10^{12}$ in five collapsed checkpoints and $-41.4$ in 55 stable
ones, using the median of checkpoint-level state medians. Episode-cluster
intervals exclude zero for critic error at 58 of 60 checkpoints, and restoring
the sampled episode clock preserves the error sign at all 60. Its action-ranking
subset is sparse, so we use it only as targeted checkpoint-level evidence.

\subsection{Target-action displacement and final-policy reach}

At the same nominal budget, BAR-Prox realizes a smaller current-state first-hop
displacement proxy than BAR-Lin once $T\ge7$ (the Supplementary Material). At
$T=20$, their median $\widehat D_{\rm critic}$ values are $0.195$ and $0.323$.
Across 90 matched environment--budget--seed cells at $T\in\{4,7,10,14,20\}$, exactly one
of BAR-Prox and BAR-Lin collapses in 11 cases. In all 11, the stable realization has both
smaller $\widehat D_{\rm critic}$ and smaller $\widehat D_{\rm final}$.
These matched cells associate smaller current-state movement with stable
trajectories; they do not isolate a causal TD-target route.

We measure Eq.~\eqref{eq:direct_target_displacement} on all 270 audited
method checkpoints. The current-state first-branch proxy closely tracks the
deterministic target-action metric ($r=.9999$). BAR-Prox $K=3$ has lower
sample-anchored target-action displacement than TD3+BC in 88 of 90 matched cells
(median ratio $.658$), while the current-state $\widehat D_{\rm final}$ proxy
is lower in only 36 of 90 (median ratio $1.057$). A few large final-policy
contractions make the arithmetic mean difference negative despite this
typical-cell expansion. Thus audited three-hop BAR exhibits the intended
asymmetric movement response rather than merely shrinking both branches.
A separate four-seed $K=4$ dump lowers target displacement in 89/90 matched
seed-0/1 comparisons with TD3+BC (median ratio $.568$) and 85/90 with $K=3$
($.950$). It measures first-hop exposure, not the final actor; the separate
24-cell frontier panel supplies the only $K=4$ final-policy comparison.
Added depth therefore continues to suppress bootstrap exposure without
establishing universal final-policy contraction. The P3/P2 direction also holds
in 36/40 cells of a four-environment seed-2/3 sensitivity (median ratio $.905$).
Yet exposure alone does not determine return: P3 loses by more than one point in
17 of the original 84 lower-exposure cells (Supplement).

A targeted Hopper-medium audit exhibits both forms of decoupling. Over
$T=4$--$10$, $K=3$ extends final-policy reach at nearly unchanged first-hop
movement; near the $T=12,14$ transition it contracts both movements and avoids
the low-return outcome. At $T=20$, both depths saturate and fail (Supplement).

\subsection{Targeted component controls}

In eight post-hoc route-shadow runs, final-route calibration error is larger in
all four-checkpoint aggregates when each shadow is evaluated under its native
continuation (mean difference $.124$, sign-flip $p=.0078$). Under a common
first-route continuation, the difference remains positive in six of eight runs
but is inconclusive by the pre-specified rule (mean $.116$, $p=.164$). The
shadows never drive the actors, so these are calibration diagnostics rather
than return interventions. Compute-matched sequential, fixed-reference, and
direct-final controls score $71.4,70.0,$ and $59.4$; slower $K=1$ target
averaging does not rescue selected cells. These controls favor routed
multi-actor refinement over added optimizer calls or target lag alone without
isolating a single sweep-wide cause (Supplement).

\subsection{Discussion and limitations}

Both realizations broaden the high-budget stability envelope, with BAR-Prox
stronger. The $K=3$ audit exhibits exposure--reach asymmetry, while $K=4$ adds
target-branch contraction and lower-tail rescue. A projection-free
frozen-critic audit supports local explicit--implicit agreement; the live result
belongs to a coupled finite-step procedure in which depth changes local step,
re-centering, actor compute, normalization, routing, and optimization history.

Both headline score grids use four seeds, and the proximal grid forms two
host-separated pairs. The full P3 movement and exposure audit uses seeds 0--1;
a restricted four-environment seed-2/3 exposure audit supplies a sensitivity
check, while the P4 target dump uses four seeds. For proximal seeds 2--3,
provenance consists of the archived launch configuration and score matrices;
exact per-run manifests were not retained, and post-hoc P4 manifests do not
reconstruct them. Only a 24-cell panel measures matched P4 final displacement.
The tasks span three dynamics families, collapse is descriptive, and controls
are post hoc. We provide no offline rule for choosing $T$ or $K$. Actor time
and memory scale as $O(K)$; the ideal fixed-critic analysis motivates but does
not certify the live chain.

\section{CONCLUSION}
\label{sec:conclusion}

BAR turns refinement depth into a practical stability control by routing the
first actor into Bellman targets and deploying the final actor. Across four
proximal seeds, the $T\ge4$ mean rises from $25.38$ at $K=1$ to $63.97$ at
$K=4$ as low-return cells fall from $34/63$ to $8/63$; both disjoint seed pairs
reproduce the ordering. Across the four-seed linearized grid, $K=3$ also gains
$8.01$ points over $K=2$, and both disjoint seed pairs reproduce the direction. Direct audits identify moderate-depth exposure--reach
decoupling and added-depth first-hop contraction, with lower-tail rescue driving
most of the latter gain. $K$ therefore controls a coupled finite-step procedure shaping bootstrap
exposure and deployment movement, not merely a discretization-accuracy knob.
