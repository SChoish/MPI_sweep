\begin{abstract}
Behavior-regularized offline actor--critic methods commonly use one policy branch both to construct bootstrapped critic targets and to form the deployed policy. This ties the target-policy displacement seen during critic training to the total reach of policy improvement. We introduce multi-step proximal policy improvement (MPI), a re-centered $K$-hop refinement of TD3+BC in which a target copy of the first hop supplies TD actions and the final hop supplies the evaluated policy. For deterministic actors, squared action anchoring defines a Wasserstein--2 proximal step. We derive a finite-step margin relative to the learned critic, an explicit critic-error transfer term, a first-hop target-perturbation bound, and a fixed-$K$ small-budget expansion. In a complete sweep over nine D4RL locomotion tasks and fourteen fixed budgets, three proximal hops reduce large-budget low-return cells from $35/63$ to $17/63$; three scale-matched linearized hops reduce them to $24/63$. Across audited checkpoints, three-hop MPI lowers deterministic target-policy displacement relative to TD3+BC in $88/90$ paired runs, while its current-state final-policy displacement proxy is lower in only $36/90$. In a paired shadow-critic intervention on eight targeted runs, bootstrapping through the final hop yields worse simulator-referenced calibration than bootstrapping through the first hop in all eight. Together with the failure diagnostics, these results show that the implemented fixed-budget refinement bundle broadens the empirical stability envelope while keeping the theoretical guarantee explicitly relative to the learned critic.
\end{abstract}
