\begin{abstract}
Offline actor--critic learning must control how far policy improvement moves before critic error feeds back through bootstrapped targets. In TD3+BC, one actor branch determines both target actions and the deployed policy. Multi-step proximal policy improvement (MPI) instead divides a nominal actor budget $T$ across $K$ persistent, behavior-anchored actors: the first branch supplies TD-target actions and the last branch is deployed. For deterministic policies, the squared anchor is a fiberwise Wasserstein--2 movement cost. We derive a conditional finite-step descent inequality relative to the learned critic and a first-hop target-perturbation bound, while distinguishing these statements from the implemented one-Adam-step realization. In a complete two-seed sweep over nine D4RL locomotion tasks and fourteen budgets, proximal MPI with $K=4$ raises the high-budget mean score from $25.97$ to $63.61$ and reduces low-return cells from $35/63$ to $9/63$. Relative to $K=3$, it rescues eight collapsed cells without creating a new collapsed cell at the two-seed cell level; the mean improvement is $10.83$ but the median is only $0.32$, locating the gain in the lower tail. A linearized realization also improves with additional hops, and the one-hop phase diagram reproduces on two further seeds. These results support an end-to-end stability effect of budget splitting. They do not isolate re-centering, routing, compute, or initialization; in particular, the released implementation changes critic initialization with $K$, which remains a required controlled rerun.
\end{abstract}
