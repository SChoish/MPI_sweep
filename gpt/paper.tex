\begin{abstract}
A squared behavior anchor in deterministic TD3+BC is a Wasserstein--2 movement cost, suggesting a minimizing-movement view in which a nominal policy-improvement time $T$ is split into $K$ steps of size $h=T/K$. We use this construction to study offline actor--critic training beyond the local gradient-flow regime. Budgeted actor refinement (BAR) implements the split with a persistent $K$-actor chain: the first actor supplies Bellman-target actions and the last is deployed. The interpretation changes with scale. At small $T$, a learned-ReLU audit shows high residence inside a fixed local affine region, where the ideal explicit and implicit updates share the same first-order flow. At finite and large $T$, the live one-Adam-step chain is no longer a certified JKO discretization, but the split creates a target/deployment asymmetry that remains measurable. Across nine D4RL locomotion tasks, fourteen budgets, and four seeds, proximal BAR raises the equally weighted $T\ge4$ mean from $25.38$ at $K=1$ to $63.97$ at $K=4$ and reduces score-$<20$ cells from $34/63$ to $8/63$. Direct audits show reduced target-action displacement without typical final-policy contraction. Yet direct two-actor controls show that sequential re-centering is not necessary for the aggregate stability gain: a locked P4 comparison gives BAR minus two-actor mean $-2.31$, task-resampling interval $[-7.25,2.41]$, and an unresolved predeclared outcome. Thus the experiments trace a progression from Wasserstein time splitting to a more general finite-step principle: conservative bootstrap-facing movement can be separated from deployment reach, while the sequential chain itself is not established as essential.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Offline reinforcement learning must improve a policy using a critic trained only from a fixed dataset. In bootstrapped actor--critic algorithms, the actor has two distinct roles: it determines which actions appear inside Bellman targets and which actions are eventually deployed. TD3+BC uses the same deterministic actor for both roles \citep{fujimoto2021minimalist}. Increasing its critic weight can therefore improve deployment reach and simultaneously move target actions into regions where the learned critic is poorly calibrated.

Our starting point is geometric rather than architectural. For deterministic policies, the squared action anchor used by TD3+BC is the statewise Wasserstein--2 distance between Dirac policies. Holding a critic fixed therefore turns a normalized TD3+BC actor objective into a minimizing-movement step
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P_h^{\bar Q}(\nu)\in\argmin_\mu\left\{-\E_s\bar Q(s,\mu(s))+\frac{1}{2h}\mathsf d_d^2(\mu,\nu)\right\},
\label{eq:intro_prox}
\end{equation}
with $h=\alpha/2$. This suggests representing a total nominal improvement time $T$ by $K$ re-centered steps of size $h=T/K$. In an ideal fixed-critic, small-step setting, this is the familiar minimizing-movement/JKO logic: repeated proximal steps approximate a Wasserstein gradient flow over total time $T$.

The offline actor--critic implementation immediately makes this question more interesting. BAR realizes the split with $K$ independently initialized persistent actors, each taking one Adam step. The first actor is anchored to dataset actions, later actors are anchored to their predecessors, only the first actor's Polyak copy enters Bellman targets, and the final actor is evaluated. Hence splitting $T$ does more than refine a numerical discretization: it also separates the branch exposed to bootstrapping from the branch used for deployment.

This leads to three empirical regimes rather than one universal JKO interpretation. In the \emph{local gradient-flow regime}, small $T$ and small realized displacement make the fixed-critic Wasserstein picture meaningful. A locked learned-ReLU audit supports the scope of this interpretation: on 6144 fresh Hopper/Walker anchors, full-horizon residence within one local affine/action-box region is $.954,.911,.842,.748$ at $T=.025,.05,.1,.2$. In the \emph{finite-step refinement regime}, persistent neural actors, changing normalization, and critic coupling break exact proximal composition, but the split still separates target-facing movement from final-policy reach. In the \emph{nonlocal stability regime} at large $T$, projection, saturation, critic drift, and optimizer history dominate; here $K$ should be interpreted as a complete stability intervention rather than a discretization-accuracy parameter.

The phase diagram is striking in precisely this nonlocal regime. Over nine D4RL locomotion tasks, fourteen budgets, and four seeds, proximal BAR gives high-budget means
\[
25.38\rightarrow41.20\rightarrow51.92\rightarrow63.97
\]
for $K=1,2,3,4$, while seed-mean score-$<20$ cells decrease from $34$ to $22$ to $16$ to $8$ out of $63$. Both disjoint seed pairs reproduce the full ordering. A projected-linearized realization also improves with depth, showing that the phenomenon is not unique to one actor loss.

The mechanism is more subtle than ``more JKO steps are better.'' Direct next-state audits show that BAR-P3 lowers target-action displacement relative to TD3+BC in $88/90$ matched cells while its typical final-policy displacement does not contract. A direct target-value audit further measures the resulting Bellman-target perturbation rather than treating movement as critic error. Yet a simpler two-actor control, with a conservative target actor and an aggressive directly data-anchored deployment actor, reproduces the P3 aggregate without sequential re-centering. In the locked P4 comparison, BAR minus two-actor has task-equal mean $-2.31$ and 95\% task-resampling interval $[-7.25,2.41]$; the predeclared outcome is unresolved. The chain therefore reveals a useful separation principle without being established as its necessary implementation.

The resulting picture is a progression:
\[
\text{Wasserstein minimizing movement}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{budget splitting}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{target--deployment separation}.
\]
The first arrow motivates BAR and is locally meaningful; the second is what survives in coupled finite-step training. This framing also clarifies the relation to MCEP \citep{xu2024mcep}: target/evaluation separation is not new, but BAR provides a depth-indexed route from a geometric refinement construction to a high-resolution stability study, together with direct movement, value-perturbation, and critic-calibration diagnostics.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item We derive BAR from a deterministic Wasserstein minimizing-movement view of behavior-regularized actor improvement, with total nominal time $T$ split into $K$ steps of size $T/K$.
\item We delimit three regimes of interpretation: local gradient-flow behavior at small $T$, finite-step target/deployment decoupling at intermediate scale, and a nonlocal stability regime where the live persistent chain is not a certified JKO discretization.
\item Four-seed phase diagrams, direct target-action and target-value audits, critic-calibration diagnostics, a learned-ReLU local-scope audit, and locked two-actor controls show that the robust large-$T$ conclusion is target--deployment separation, while the incremental return value of sequential re-centering remains unresolved.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Offline actor--critic stability.}
Offline value learning is vulnerable to bootstrapping on actions outside the data-supported region \citep{fujimoto2019offpolicy,kumar2019bear}. TD3+BC controls this with a squared cloning penalty and normalized critic objective \citep{fujimoto2021minimalist}. One-step offline RL argues that repeated optimization against an off-policy critic can itself be harmful \citep{brandfonbrener2021offline}. We instead keep iterative TD3-style training and study how splitting a nominal improvement time changes both local refinement and target/deployment dataflow.

\paragraph{Target/evaluation policy separation.}
MCEP uses a conservative target policy for value learning and a less constrained evaluation policy excluded from Bellman backups \citep{xu2024mcep}. BAR shares this routing principle but was constructed as a persistent predecessor-recentered chain with depth varied under a fixed nominal budget. Our controls show that direct two-actor separation can reproduce much of the observed stability, so we do not claim that the target/evaluation split itself is new. The empirical contribution is the depth phase diagram, direct movement/value diagnostics, and the controlled comparison between sequential and direct separation.

\paragraph{Policy geometry and minimizing movements.}
Trust-region methods are commonly motivated by information geometry \citep{amari2000methods,schulman2015trust}. Wasserstein formulations instead view policy improvement as transport or gradient flow \citep{zhang2018policy,moskovitz2020efficient,terpin2022trust,song2023metric}. Wasserstein Policy Optimization projects value-gradient flow into a parametric actor class \citep{pfau2025wpo}. The minimizing-movement/JKO viewpoint approximates metric gradient flow by repeated proximal steps \citep{ambrosio2005gradient,otto2001geometry}. For deterministic policies, squared action displacement is exactly the statewise $W_2^2$ distance between Dirac policies. We use this identity as the derivational origin of BAR, while explicitly restricting gradient-flow claims to the local fixed-critic regime.

\section{Method and Interpretation}
We consider a fixed dataset $\D=\{(s_i,a_i,r_i,s_i')\}_{i=1}^N$ and twin target critics. Standard TD3+BC uses
\begin{equation}
y=r+\gamma\min_jQ_j^-(s',\mu^-(s'))
\end{equation}
and minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\rm TD3BC}=-\alpha\E_s\bar Q(s,\mu(s))+\E_{(s,a)\sim\D}\frac{\|\mu(s)-a\|_2^2}{d},
\end{equation}
where $\bar Q=\widehat Q/C$ and $C$ is a stop-gradient critic scale.

\subsection{From TD3+BC to a Wasserstein minimizing movement}
For deterministic policies and state distribution $\rho$, define
\begin{equation}
\mathsf d_{d,\rho}^2(\mu,\nu)=\E_{s\sim\rho}\frac{\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2}{d}.
\label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
Because $W_2^2(\delta_{\mu(s)},\delta_{\nu(s)})=\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2$, this is a rescaled integrated squared Wasserstein distance. Strictly, it is a pseudometric on pointwise policy maps and a metric modulo $\rho$-almost-everywhere equality. At population level, the squared dataset anchor equals the squared distance to the conditional-mean behavior map plus a policy-independent conditional-variance term.

For a fixed normalized critic, Eq.~\eqref{eq:intro_prox} is therefore the ideal proximal update corresponding to the anchored actor objective, with $h=\alpha/2$. If the ideal map is consistent and the critic is fixed, splitting a total time $T$ into $K$ steps gives the minimizing-movement composition
\begin{equation}
\mu_K^{\rm ideal}=\bigl(\mathcal P_{T/K}^{\bar Q}\bigr)^K(\mu_0),
\label{eq:jko_comp}
\end{equation}
which approaches the associated metric gradient flow as the step size vanishes under the usual local regularity assumptions. This is the origin of the $T/K$ construction, not a claim that the trained neural chain exactly solves Eq.~\eqref{eq:jko_comp}.

\subsection{Budgeted actor refinement}
BAR maintains $K$ independently initialized persistent actors. Let $T=\alpha/2$ and $h=T/K$. On each delayed actor update, $\mu_1$ takes one TD3+BC-style Adam step toward dataset actions,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_1=-2h\E_s\bar Q_1(s,\mu_1(s))+\E_{(s,a)\sim\D}\frac{\|\mu_1(s)-a\|^2}{d}.
\end{equation}
For $k\ge2$, defining $\nu_k=\sg\mu_{k-1}^{+}$ on the same minibatch,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_k=-2h\E_s\bar Q_k(s,\mu_k(s))+\E_s\frac{\|\mu_k(s)-\nu_k(s)\|^2}{d}.
\end{equation}
Only $\mu_1^-$ supplies Bellman-target actions; $\mu_K$ is deployed. Increasing $K$ jointly changes local coefficient, re-centering frequency, actor compute, normalization trajectory, and target/deployment dataflow. Hence each $K$ is a complete procedure, not an isolated discretization change.

\subsection{Three regimes of interpretation}
The same $T/K$ construction has different meanings at different scales.
