\begin{abstract}
TD3+BC uses the same actor branch to construct bootstrapped critic targets and to produce the deployed policy, coupling target-action movement to deployment reach. We study \emph{budgeted actor refinement} (\method), a persistent actor chain that divides a fixed nominal actor-loss coefficient budget across $K$ local updates: a Polyak copy of the first actor supplies TD-target actions, while the final actor is deployed. For deterministic actors, the squared action anchors admit an ideal Wasserstein--2 proximal interpretation. We derive a conditional learned-critic improvement margin and a first-hop target-perturbation bound, while explicitly separating these ideal statements from the live one-Adam-step implementation. Across nine D4RL locomotion tasks, fourteen coefficient budgets, and two seeds, proximal refinement at $K=4$ raises the high-budget mean from $25.97$ to $63.61$ and reduces score-$<20$ cells from $35/63$ to $9/63$. The $K=4$ minus $K=3$ mean gain is $10.83$, but its median is only $.32$, locating the gain mainly in lower-tail rescue. Descriptive task-cluster intervals are $[20.51,54.35]$ for $K=4$ versus TD3+BC and $[2.23,21.97]$ versus $K=3$. In a 270-checkpoint audit, three-hop refinement lowers sample-anchored next-state target-action displacement in $88/90$ matched comparisons with TD3+BC, whereas the current-state final-policy proxy is lower in only $36/90$. These results establish a strong end-to-end effect for the tested refinement bundle, not a causal effect of routing, re-centering, or added actor compute alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Offline reinforcement learning (RL) learns from a fixed dataset without additional environment interaction. Its difficulty is not only selecting apparently high-value actions, but also determining how aggressively a policy may be improved before the learned critic becomes unreliable. In TD3+BC, a deterministic actor maximizes a scale-normalized critic while remaining close to dataset actions; a Polyak copy of the same actor supplies actions in Bellman targets \citep{fujimoto2021minimalist}. Consequently, the policy movement seen by critic training and the reach of the evaluated policy are controlled by one branch and one coefficient.

We investigate this coupling through \method, a persistent chain of $K$ actors. The first actor is anchored to sampled dataset actions. Each later actor is anchored to the stop-gradient action of its predecessor on the same minibatch. The first actor's Polyak target copy enters the TD target, whereas the final actor is evaluated (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). A total nominal coefficient budget $T$ is divided as $h=T/K$ across the chain. Here, \emph{budget} means the sum of actor-loss coefficients: $T$ is neither a compute budget nor a realized policy-distance constraint. Increasing $K$ is a meaningful refinement-depth intervention, but it also changes local coefficient, persistent actor count, actor optimizer calls, re-centering frequency, normalization trajectory, and target--deployment dataflow. Cross-$K$ comparisons therefore estimate the end-to-end effect of the complete procedure at each tested depth.

We use \emph{stability envelope} operationally. On the tested grid, a high-budget environment--coefficient cell is called collapsed when its two-seed mean D4RL normalized score is below $20$; the empirical stability envelope is the complement of these cells. This is a locomotion failure indicator, not a theoretical threshold. The full grid shows a systematic expansion of this envelope with proximal refinement depth, replicated qualitatively by a projected linearized realization. The gain is highly nonuniform: the transition from $K=3$ to $K=4$ has a large mean but near-zero median improvement, indicating rescue of a lower tail rather than a generic score increase.

The geometric analysis is deliberately narrower than the algorithmic claim. A squared anchor between deterministic actions is an integrated Wasserstein--2 cost, so an exactly solved, fixed-critic subproblem has a proximal interpretation. This yields a conditional learned-critic margin and a local explicit--implicit relationship. The live method, however, maintains independently initialized persistent actors and applies one Adam update to each. Even at $h=0$, a later actor may have a nonzero anchoring gradient when it differs from its predecessor. Thus the ideal proximal map motivates the architecture but does not certify the actual optimizer chain.

Our contributions are threefold. First, we formulate routed multi-actor refinement for TD3+BC and specify its implementation-level normalization and dataflow. Second, we provide an ideal proximal analysis with explicit conditions and a first-hop TD-target perturbation bound. Third, complete fixed-budget sweeps, task-cluster uncertainty, direct target-action audits, critic-failure diagnostics, and targeted controls show that the tested bundle broadens the large-budget stability envelope primarily by preventing catastrophic failures.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Behavior-regularized offline RL.}
BCQ and BEAR restrict policy actions to limit bootstrapping error under distribution shift \citep{fujimoto2019offpolicy,kumar2019bear}. TD3+BC instead adds a squared behavior-cloning term to a normalized deterministic actor objective \citep{fujimoto2021minimalist}. One-step offline RL argues that repeated optimization against off-policy value estimates can amplify estimation error and that a single regularized improvement step can be competitive \citep{brandfonbrener2021offline}. \method\ retains iterative actor--critic training but distributes a nominal improvement coefficient across a routed actor chain. It therefore studies a different intervention: how refinement depth and branch assignment alter the failure envelope of a fixed TD3+BC backbone.

\paragraph{Geometric policy optimization.}
Trust-region methods usually use information-geometric discrepancies \citep{schulman2015trust}. Wasserstein formulations instead view policy optimization as transport or gradient flow \citep{zhang2018policy,moskovitz2021efficient,terpin2022trust,song2023metric}. Most closely, Wasserstein Policy Optimization projects Wasserstein policy flow into a parametric actor class and uses action gradients of the value function \citep{pfau2025wpo}. Our contribution is not the deterministic action-gradient identity itself. We study an offline behavior-anchored persistent chain whose early and terminal actors have different roles in Bellman targets and deployment, and we audit the resulting large-coefficient failures. The proximal analysis below concerns an ideal fixed-critic operator; the empirical contribution concerns the coupled training bundle.

\section{Method}
We consider a fixed dataset $\D=\{(s_i,a_i,r_i,s_i')\}_{i=1}^N$ with continuous action dimension $d$. A clipped double critic uses
\begin{equation}
 y_\rho=r+\gamma(1-done)\min_{j\in\{1,2\}}Q_j^-(s',\rho(s')).
 \label{eq:tdtarget}
\end{equation}
At each delayed policy update, the online critic is frozen while the actor chain is updated in order on one minibatch. Let $\widehat Q$ denote the online first critic and $h=T/K$.

\paragraph{Proximal realization.}
The first actor minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{1}=-\frac{2h}{C_{1}^{\mathrm P}}\E_s\widehat Q(s,\mu_1(s))
 +\E_{(s,a)\sim\D}\frac{\|\mu_1(s)-a\|_2^2}{d},
 \label{eq:firstloss}
\end{equation}
using one Adam step. For $k\ge2$, with $\nu_k(s)=\sg\mu_{k-1}^{+}(s)$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{k}=-\frac{2h}{C_{k}^{\mathrm P}}\E_s\widehat Q(s,\mu_k(s))
 +\E_s\frac{\|\mu_k(s)-\nu_k(s)\|_2^2}{d}.
 \label{eq:laterloss}
\end{equation}
The superscripts ``pre'' and ``$+$'' denote actions before and after the current actor update. The implementation-level scale factors are
\begin{align}
 C_{1,t}^{\mathrm P}&=\sg\!\left(\E_s|\widehat Q_t(s,\mu_{1,t}^{\mathrm{pre}}(s))|+\epsilon\right),\label{eq:cprox1}\\
 C_{k,t}^{\mathrm P}&=\sg\!\left(\E_s|\widehat Q_t(s,\sg\mu_{k-1,t}^{+}(s))|+\epsilon\right),\ k\ge2.\label{eq:cproxk}
\end{align}
After the first actor update, its target copy and the critic target are Polyak-updated; later actors do not enter Eq.~\eqref{eq:tdtarget}.

\paragraph{Projected linearized realization.}
For comparison, a persistent actor regresses in one MSE step to
\begin{equation}
 a_{k}^{\rm tar}=\Pi_{\A}\!\left[\nu_k+d h\frac{\nabla_a\widehat Q(s,\nu_k)}{C_{k}^{\mathrm L}}\right],
 \label{eq:linear}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_1=a$ and $\nu_k=\sg\mu_{k-1}^{+}$ for $k\ge2$. The factor $d$ matches the per-coordinate action metric, but the first-hop normalization point differs from the proximal realization:
\begin{align}
 C_{1,t}^{\mathrm L}&=\sg\!\left(\E_{(s,a)\sim\D}|\widehat Q_t(s,a)|+\epsilon\right),\label{eq:clin1}\\
 C_{k,t}^{\mathrm L}&=C_{k,t}^{\mathrm P},\qquad k\ge2.\label{eq:clink}
\end{align}
We therefore call this an \emph{action-metric-matched projected linearization}, not a completely scale-matched control.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One delayed \method\ actor update}
\label{alg:bar}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE minibatch $B$, actors $\mu_{1:K}$, critic $\widehat Q$, $h=T/K$
\STATE Update $\mu_1$ once using Eq.~\eqref{eq:firstloss}; Polyak-update $\mu_1^-$ and $Q^-$
\FOR{$k=2,\ldots,K$}
  \STATE Set $\nu_k\leftarrow\sg\mu_{k-1}^{+}(s)$ on the same $B$
  \STATE Update $\mu_k$ once using Eq.~\eqref{eq:laterloss}
\ENDFOR
\STATE Use $\mu_1^-$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:tdtarget}; deploy $\mu_K$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Ideal Proximal Interpretation}
For deterministic policies and a state distribution $\rho$, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf d_{d,\rho}^2(\mu,\nu)=\E_{s\sim\rho}\frac{\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2}{d}.
 \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
Because $W_2^2(\delta_{\mu(s)},\delta_{\nu(s)})=\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2$, this is a rescaled integrated squared Wasserstein distance. Moreover, if $b(s)=\E[a\mid s]$, the population squared behavior anchor equals $\mathsf d_{d,\rho_B}^2(\mu,b)$ plus conditional action variance.

Given a fixed positive scale $C_k$ and reference $\nu$, define the ideal operator
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_h^{\widehat Q}(\nu)\in\argmin_{\mu\in\M}
 \left\{-\E_s\frac{\widehat Q(s,\mu(s))}{C_k}
 +\frac{1}{2h}\mathsf d_d^2(\mu,\nu)\right\}.
 \label{eq:prox}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:firstloss}--\eqref{eq:laterloss} are one-optimizer-step approximations to this objective, with sampled actions in the first hop and persistent neural actors thereafter.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional learned-critic margin]
Suppose the reference $\nu_k$ is feasible for hop $k$ and the computed actor satisfies
$\mathcal L_k(\mu_k\mid\nu_k)\le \mathcal L_k(\nu_k\mid\nu_k)+\varepsilon_k$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{2h}{C_k}\E_s[\widehat Q(s,\mu_k)-\widehat Q(s,\nu_k)]
 -\mathsf d_d^2(\mu_k,\nu_k)\ge-\varepsilon_k.
 \label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
This statement is conditional on realized objective decrease. For the sample-anchored first hop, an individual dataset action is not generally a feasible deterministic-policy comparator; the population conditional-mean behavior map or an explicit behavior actor is required for a literal policy-class certificate. If $\widehat Q=Q^\dagger+e$, transferring Eq.~\eqref{eq:margin} to a trusted objective adds the pathwise error difference
$-(2h/C_k)\E[e(s,\mu_k)-e(s,\nu_k)]$. It does not imply monotone environment return.

\begin{proposition}[First-hop target perturbation]
Assume each target critic $Q_j^-(s',\cdot)$ is $L(s')$-Lipschitz. For noiseless targets and any reference $\nu$,
\begin{equation}
 \|y_{\mu_1}-y_\nu\|_{L_2(\D)}
 \le \gamma\!\left(\E_{s'}L(s')^2\|\mu_1(s')-\nu(s')\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:perturb}
\end{equation}
If $L(s')\le\bar L$, the bound is $\bar L\gamma\sqrt{dD_{\rm critic}}$. Conditional on $\mu_1$, later actors do not enter this one-step bound.
\end{proposition}
With common target-smoothing noise, action clipping is nonexpansive and the same comparison applies. The result bounds a target-value difference, not critic stability.

\paragraph{Local flow motivation, not a live guarantee.}
For an interior action, fixed critic, exact statewise solve, and $\beta=dh/C$, the projected-linearized step is $\Delta a_{\rm lin}=\beta g$, while the ideal proximal stationary equation is $\Delta a_{\rm prox}=\beta\nabla Q(a_0+\Delta a_{\rm prox})$. If the Hessian is locally Lipschitz and $\beta\|H\|<1$,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta a_{\rm prox}=\beta g+\beta^2Hg+O(\beta^3).
 \label{eq:local}
\end{equation}
Thus the two ideal updates are first-order discretizations of the same local action flow. This does not transfer automatically to persistent ReLU actors trained by one Adam step: the actual later-actor map need not approach the identity as $h\to0$. Our frozen-critic audit checks ideal action-level consistency, not the live optimizer chain. The empirical method should therefore be read as a fixed-nominal-budget refinement bundle whose behavior is locally motivated, but not certified, by Eq.~\eqref{eq:prox}.

For completeness, if an \emph{ideal} smooth map satisfies $\Psi_h(x)=x+h f(x)+h^2p(x)+O(h^3)$, then for fixed $K$ and $T\to0$,
\begin{align}
\Psi_{T/K}^{K}(x_0)
={}&x_0+Tf_0\\
&+T^2\left[\frac{K-1}{2K}J_0f_0+\frac{1}{K}p_0\right]+O(T^3).
\label{eq:composition}
\end{align}
Explicit Euler has $p_0=0$ and exact backward Euler has $p_0=J_0f_0$, yielding ideal leading flow bias proportional to $1/K$. Equation~\eqref{eq:composition} is fixed-depth and conditional on the near-identity premise; it is not a uniform $K\to\infty$ result or a theorem for the live actor networks.

\section{Experiments}
\paragraph{Protocol.}
We use the nine D4RL-v2 locomotion datasets formed by HalfCheetah, Hopper, and Walker2d with \texttt{medium}, \texttt{medium-replay}, and \texttt{expert} data. The exact IDs are listed in the supplement. We sweep
$T\in\{.05,.1,.2,.4,.7,1.5,2.5,4,7,10,12,14,17,20\}$
for TD3+BC ($K=1$), proximal $K\in\{2,3,4\}$, and linearized $K\in\{2,3\}$, using two seeds per cell and $10^6$ critic updates. Archived runs were evaluated every $50{,}000$ critic updates; every reported sweep score is the ten-episode normalized mean at the final $10^6$-update checkpoint. All conditions share the dataset, critic architecture, and number of critic updates, but a depth-$K$ run performs $K$ actor optimizer calls per delayed update.

\subsection{Refinement Depth Expands the Tested Envelope}
Table~\ref{tab:main} and Figure~\ref{fig:stability} show a broad high-budget improvement with proximal depth. Relative to TD3+BC, $K=4$ gains $37.64$ points on average over tasks; the descriptive task-cluster 95\% interval is $[20.51,54.35]$, and eight of nine task means improve. Relative to $K=3$, the mean gain is $10.83$ with interval $[2.23,21.97]$. However, the cellwise median gain is only $.32$. At the score-20 threshold, eight $K=3$ collapsed cells become stable at $K=4$ and none move in the opposite direction. The raw-run collapse-risk difference between $K=4$ and $K=3$ is approximately $-.095$; its task-cluster interval $[-.206,0]$ reaches zero, so a strict collapse-probability improvement over $K=3$ remains statistically inconclusive.

The linearized realization moves in the same direction: $K=2\to3$ raises the high-budget mean from $36.68$ to $43.91$ and reduces collapsed cells from 29 to 24. Proximal refinement remains stronger overall, so this is evidence about complete training procedures rather than an isolated implicit-versus-explicit integrator effect. At $T=20$, raw linearized targets reach the action box in roughly 94--95\% of audited states and effective coefficients are heavily attenuated; the large-budget regime is therefore projected, amortized, and critic-coupled rather than a clean local discretization experiment.

The per-task pattern rules out a universal monotone frontier law. Hopper-M and Walker2d-E exhibit large relocated stability windows, Hopper-MR saturates near the dataset ceiling, and HalfCheetah-M remains insensitive to depth. Comparable nominal budgets or first-hop movements can therefore correspond to different outcomes across tasks; the phase diagram is a task-conditioned stability map rather than evidence for one global critic-valid radius.

The $K=1$ phase diagram reproduces on independent seeds 2--3: the high-budget mean is $24.79$ with $35/63$ collapsed cells, compared with $25.97$ and $35/63$ on seeds 0--1. A targeted four-seed Hopper-medium check for $K=4$ gives $100.9\pm1.7$ at $T=17$ and $41.8\pm44.0$ at $T=20$. Because $K=3$ retains only two seeds there, this validates the $K=4$ window rather than providing a four-seed cross-$K$ comparison. The ordering is also insensitive to the exact failure threshold: for thresholds 10, 20, 30, and 40, P4 has respectively 9, 9, 11, and 12 collapsed high-budget cells, compared with 29, 35, 36, and 39 for TD3+BC. These are descriptive robustness checks rather than multiple confirmatory tests.

\subsection{Movement and Critic-Failure Audits}
We distinguish three empirical quantities. On a current-state validation batch,
$\widehat D_{\rm critic}=\E\|\mu_1(s_i)-a_i\|^2/d$ and
$\widehat D_{\rm final}=\E\|\mu_K(s_i)-a_i\|^2/d$ use online actors. On bootstrapped next-state pairs,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat D_{\rm target}=\E_i\|\mu_1^-(s_i')-a_{i+1}\|_2^2/d
 \label{eq:dtarget}
\end{equation}
uses the Polyak target actor; a smoothed version averages common TD3 noise draws. Equation~\eqref{eq:dtarget} is a sample-anchored next-state target-action displacement, not a direct policy-to-policy Wasserstein distance. It and $\widehat D_{\rm final}$ also use different states, anchors, and Polyak status.

Across 270 audited checkpoints, the current-state first-branch proxy correlates $.9999$ with deterministic Eq.~\eqref{eq:dtarget}. Three-hop proximal refinement has lower target-action displacement than TD3+BC in $88/90$ matched environment--budget--seed cells, with median ratio $.658$; the 18-cluster interval for the lower fraction is $[.944,1]$. Against $K=2$, it is lower in $84/90$ cells with median ratio $.903$. In contrast, $\widehat D_{\rm final}$ is lower than TD3+BC in only $36/90$ cells and has median ratio $1.057$. A few large contractions make its arithmetic mean difference negative, so the accurate conclusion is heterogeneous final-policy movement rather than universal expansion. Moreover, among the 84 cases with lower target displacement than $K=2$, $K=3$ loses by more than one score point in 17; exposure alone does not determine return.

Collapsed runs exhibit orders-of-magnitude larger TD-error tails, critic magnitudes, and critic losses (Figure~\ref{fig:failure}). Under a frozen TD3+BC $T=1$ critic matched by environment and seed, median run-total actor-chain gain changes from $+2.11$ to $-1.42$ for stable versus collapsed linearized runs, and from $+2.51$ to $-1.06$ for proximal runs. This sign reversal diagnoses cross-critic disagreement; the common critic is not ground truth. In a simulator screening, the median of checkpoint-level signed-error medians, $\widehat Q_1(s,a_K)-Q_{\rm MC}^{\rho_1}(s,a_K)$, is $-41.4$ over 55 stable checkpoints and $1.70\times10^{12}$ over five collapsed checkpoints. The ranking subset is sparse and is reported only in the supplement.

\subsection{Targeted Controls and Limitations}
Two actor-detached shadow critics isolate backup route while sharing parameters, minibatches, target noise, and a fixed actor trajectory. The four environment--budget cells were selected post hoc at visible transition regions. Against the common first-route continuation, final-route error is larger in all eight four-checkpoint run averages under the primary state-median aggregation, but only in five of eight final-checkpoint comparisons. This measures alignment to the primary first-route estimand, not each route critic's own-continuation calibration; neither shadow critic drives the actor, so it is not a routing effect on return.

In the same targeted eight-run set, sequential $K=3$, fixed-reference $K=3$, and a direct-final compute-matched control score $71.4$, $70.0$, and $59.4$ on average, with collapse counts $2/8$, $2/8$, and $3/8$. Thus the sample does not identify re-centering alone. Slowing only the one-hop actor-target Polyak rate by three gives $7/8$ collapses versus $6/8$ under the standard rate and does not reproduce the three-hop rescue. These controls narrow simple explanations but do not decompose the full sweep effect.

The principal limitations are the two-seed complete grid, nine datasets from only three dynamics families, a descriptive collapse threshold, and targeted post-hoc mechanism studies. We do not provide an offline rule for choosing $T$ or $K$. Actor-update time and actor memory scale as $O(K)$, while critic updates and dataset sample count are fixed. Most importantly, the theoretical statements apply to ideal fixed-critic subproblems under explicit regularity and optimization conditions; they do not guarantee the independently initialized, persistent one-Adam-step actor chain.

\section{Conclusion}
\method\ splits a nominal actor-improvement coefficient across persistent TD3+BC actors, routes the first actor to Bellman targets, and deploys the final actor. On a complete two-seed phase diagram, deeper proximal refinement substantially broadens the tested large-budget stability envelope, with the strongest gains concentrated in catastrophic-failure rescue. Direct audits confirm a consistent reduction in sample-anchored next-state target-action displacement without a corresponding typical-cell contraction of the final-policy proxy. The evidence supports the stability of the implemented refinement bundle. The Wasserstein analysis supplies a local ideal interpretation, not a certificate for the live optimizer chain, and the existing controls do not causally isolate routing, re-centering, or added actor compute.
