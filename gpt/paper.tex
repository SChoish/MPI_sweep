\begin{abstract}
TD3+BC uses one actor branch both to construct bootstrapped critic targets and to produce the deployed policy, forcing bootstrap exposure and policy reach to move together. We introduce \emph{budgeted actor refinement} (\method), a persistent $K$-actor chain that separates these roles: a Polyak copy of the first actor supplies TD-target actions, while the final actor is deployed. A finite-step Lipschitz bound shows that one-step target perturbation depends on the first-hop displacement independently of later refinement reach; an ideal Wasserstein--2 proximal analysis characterizes the complementary local geometry. Across two disjoint, host-separated seed pairs on nine D4RL locomotion tasks and fourteen coefficient budgets, the four-seed, equally task-and-budget-weighted proximal aggregate over $T\ge4$ is strictly ordered by depth: the mean rises from $25.38$ at $K=1$ to $63.97$ at $K=4$, while score-$<20$ seed-mean cells fall from $34/63$ to $8/63$. Each seed pair separately reproduces the full $K=1<2<3<4$ aggregate ordering. The $K=4$ minus $K=3$ gain is $12.06$ with a task-resampling interval of $[3.84,21.11]$; its $.34$ cellwise median and eight rescued collapses show that low-score rescue drives most of the added-depth aggregate gain. In a 270-checkpoint $K=3$ audit, refinement lowers sample-anchored next-state target-action displacement in $88/90$ matched comparisons, while the typical final-policy proxy does not contract. Together, these results establish refinement depth as a consequential stability control and identify finite-step bootstrap-exposure decoupling as \method's central empirical mechanism signature.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Offline reinforcement learning (RL) learns from a fixed dataset without additional environment interaction. Its difficulty is not only selecting apparently high-value actions, but also determining how aggressively a policy may be improved before the learned critic becomes unreliable. In TD3+BC, a deterministic actor maximizes a scale-normalized critic while remaining close to dataset actions; a Polyak copy of the same actor supplies actions in Bellman targets \citep{fujimoto2021minimalist}. Consequently, the policy movement seen by critic training and the reach of the evaluated policy are controlled by one branch and one coefficient.

We investigate this coupling through \method, a persistent actor chain whose depth $K$ is the central experimental intervention. The first actor is anchored to sampled dataset actions, and each later actor performs a re-centered refinement around the stop-gradient action of its predecessor. The first actor's Polyak target copy enters the TD target, whereas the final actor is deployed (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). In the implementation, $T$ is the nominal TD3+BC integration-time parameter $\tau=\alpha/2$. Thus $K=1$ recovers the baseline coefficient $\alpha=2T$, while depth $K$ assigns $h=T/K$ and normalized critic coefficient $2h/C_k$ to each hop. $T$ is neither a compute budget nor a realized policy-distance constraint. Each $K$ defines a complete randomized refinement procedure, so cross-$K$ seed averages estimate the marginal end-to-end performance of depth-indexed BAR.

We use \emph{stability envelope} operationally. On the tested grid, a high-budget environment--coefficient cell is called collapsed when its within-method seed mean D4RL normalized score is below $20$; the empirical stability envelope is the complement of these cells. This is a locomotion failure indicator, not a theoretical threshold. The full grid shows a systematic expansion of this envelope with proximal refinement depth, reproduced separately in both host-separated seed pairs and directionally by a projected linearized realization. The gain is highly nonuniform: the transition from $K=3$ to $K=4$ has a large mean but near-zero median improvement, indicating rescue of a lower tail rather than a generic score increase.

BAR's central structural property is finite-step: only the first actor enters Bellman targets, so later refinements can extend deployment reach without directly increasing the displacement seen by critic bootstrapping. Under an action-Lipschitz target critic, the resulting target perturbation is controlled by first-hop displacement independently of the final actor. The squared anchors also admit an integrated Wasserstein--2 interpretation for exactly solved fixed-critic subproblems, supplying a complementary local geometric account of refinement.

Our contributions are threefold. First, we formulate routed multi-actor refinement for TD3+BC, making $K$ a practical control that separates critic-facing and deployed actor branches. Second, we establish a finite-step bootstrap-exposure bound and connect the squared anchors to ideal Wasserstein-proximal refinement under explicit conditions. Third, complete fixed-budget sweeps over two disjoint, host-separated seed pairs, direct target-action audits, critic-failure diagnostics, and targeted route controls show that depth broadens the large-budget stability envelope primarily through low-score rescue.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Behavior-regularized offline RL.}
BCQ and BEAR restrict policy actions to limit bootstrapping error under distribution shift \citep{fujimoto2019offpolicy,kumar2019bear}. TD3+BC instead adds a squared behavior-cloning term to a normalized deterministic actor objective \citep{fujimoto2021minimalist}. One-step offline RL argues that repeated optimization against off-policy value estimates can amplify estimation error and that a single regularized improvement step can be competitive \citep{brandfonbrener2021offline}. \method\ retains iterative actor--critic training but distributes a nominal improvement coefficient across a routed actor chain. It therefore studies a different intervention: how refinement depth and branch assignment alter the failure envelope of a fixed TD3+BC backbone.

\paragraph{Geometric policy optimization.}
Trust-region methods usually use information-geometric discrepancies \citep{schulman2015trust}. Wasserstein formulations instead view policy optimization as transport or gradient flow \citep{zhang2018policy,moskovitz2021efficient,terpin2022trust,song2023metric}. Most closely, Wasserstein Policy Optimization projects Wasserstein policy flow into a parametric actor class and uses action gradients of the value function \citep{pfau2025wpo}. \method\ brings Wasserstein-motivated refinement into offline bootstrapped actor--critic training: early and terminal actors have distinct target and deployment roles, and $K$ controls the refinement depth between them. The action-gradient identity is shared background; the routed depth-indexed procedure and its large-coefficient stability audit are our contribution.

\section{Method}
We consider a fixed dataset $\D=\{(s_i,a_i,r_i,s_i')\}_{i=1}^N$ with continuous action dimension $d$. A clipped double critic uses
\begin{equation}
 y_\rho=r+\gamma(1-done)\min_{j\in\{1,2\}}Q_j^-(s',\rho(s')).
 \label{eq:tdtarget}
\end{equation}
At each delayed policy update, the online critic is frozen while the actor chain is updated in order on one minibatch. Let $\widehat Q$ denote the online first critic and $h=T/K$. Unless otherwise marked, $\E_s$ is the empirical mean over states in that minibatch; the scale safeguard is $\epsilon=10^{-6}$.

\paragraph{Proximal realization.}
The first actor minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{1}=-\frac{2h}{C_{1}^{\mathrm P}}\E_s\widehat Q(s,\mu_1(s))
 +\E_{(s,a)\sim\D}\frac{\|\mu_1(s)-a\|_2^2}{d},
 \label{eq:firstloss}
\end{equation}
using one Adam step. For $k\ge2$, with $\nu_k(s)=\sg\mu_{k-1}^{+}(s)$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{k}=-\frac{2h}{C_{k}^{\mathrm P}}\E_s\widehat Q(s,\mu_k(s))
 +\E_s\frac{\|\mu_k(s)-\nu_k(s)\|_2^2}{d}.
 \label{eq:laterloss}
\end{equation}
The superscripts ``pre'' and ``$+$'' denote actions before and after the current actor update. The implementation-level scale factors are
\begin{align}
 C_{1,t}^{\mathrm P}&=\sg\!\left(\E_s|\widehat Q_t(s,\mu_{1,t}^{\mathrm{pre}}(s))|+\epsilon\right),\label{eq:cprox1}\\
 C_{k,t}^{\mathrm P}&=\sg\!\left(\E_s|\widehat Q_t(s,\sg\mu_{k-1,t}^{+}(s))|+\epsilon\right),\ k\ge2.\label{eq:cproxk}
\end{align}
After the first actor update, its target copy and the critic target are Polyak-updated; later actors do not enter Eq.~\eqref{eq:tdtarget}.

\paragraph{Projected linearized realization.}
For comparison, a persistent actor regresses in one MSE step to
\begin{equation}
 a_{k}^{\rm tar}=\Pi_{\A}\!\left[\nu_k+d h\frac{\nabla_a\widehat Q(s,\nu_k)}{C_{k}^{\mathrm L}}\right],
 \label{eq:linear}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_1=a$ and $\nu_k=\sg\mu_{k-1}^{+}$ for $k\ge2$. The factor $d$ matches the per-coordinate action metric, but the first-hop normalization point differs from the proximal realization:
\begin{align}
 C_{1,t}^{\mathrm L}&=\sg\!\left(\E_{(s,a)\sim\D}|\widehat Q_t(s,a)|+\epsilon\right),\label{eq:clin1}\\
 C_{k,t}^{\mathrm L}&=C_{k,t}^{\mathrm P},\qquad k\ge2.\label{eq:clink}
\end{align}
We therefore call this an \emph{action-metric-matched projected linearization}, not a completely scale-matched control.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One delayed \method\ actor update}
\label{alg:bar}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE minibatch $B$, actors $\mu_{1:K}$, critic $\widehat Q$, $h=T/K$
\STATE Update $\mu_1$ once using Eq.~\eqref{eq:firstloss}; Polyak-update $\mu_1^-$ and $Q^-$
\FOR{$k=2,\ldots,K$}
  \STATE Set $\nu_k\leftarrow\sg\mu_{k-1}^{+}(s)$ on the same $B$
  \STATE Update $\mu_k$ once using Eq.~\eqref{eq:laterloss}
\ENDFOR
\STATE Use $\mu_1^-$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:tdtarget}; deploy $\mu_K$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Ideal Proximal Interpretation}
For deterministic policies and a state distribution $\rho$, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf d_{d,\rho}^2(\mu,\nu)=\E_{s\sim\rho}\frac{\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2}{d}.
 \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
Because $W_2^2(\delta_{\mu(s)},\delta_{\nu(s)})=\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2$, this is a rescaled integrated squared Wasserstein distance. We write $\mathsf d_d=\mathsf d_{d,\rho}$ when the state distribution is clear. Moreover, if $b(s)=\E[a\mid s]$ and $\rho_B$ is the dataset state marginal, the population squared behavior anchor equals $\mathsf d_{d,\rho_B}^2(\mu,b)$ plus conditional action variance.

Given a fixed positive scale $C_k$ and reference $\nu$, define the ideal operator
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_h^{\widehat Q}(\nu)\in\argmin_{\mu\in\M}
 \left\{-\E_s\frac{\widehat Q(s,\mu(s))}{C_k}
 +\frac{1}{2h}\mathsf d_d^2(\mu,\nu)\right\}.
 \label{eq:prox}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:firstloss}--\eqref{eq:laterloss} are one-optimizer-step approximations to this objective, with sampled actions in the first hop and persistent neural actors thereafter.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional learned-critic margin]
Suppose the reference $\nu_k$ is feasible for hop $k$ and the computed actor satisfies
$\mathcal L_k(\mu_k\mid\nu_k)\le \mathcal L_k(\nu_k\mid\nu_k)+\varepsilon_k$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{2h}{C_k}\E_s[\widehat Q(s,\mu_k)-\widehat Q(s,\nu_k)]
 -\mathsf d_d^2(\mu_k,\nu_k)\ge-\varepsilon_k.
 \label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
This statement is conditional on realized objective decrease. For the sample-anchored first hop, an individual dataset action is not generally a feasible deterministic-policy comparator; the population conditional-mean behavior map or an explicit behavior actor is required for a literal policy-class certificate. If $\widehat Q=Q^\dagger+e$, transferring Eq.~\eqref{eq:margin} to a trusted objective adds the pathwise error difference
$-(2h/C_k)\E[e(s,\mu_k)-e(s,\nu_k)]$. It does not imply monotone environment return.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-step bootstrap-exposure bound]
Assume each target critic $Q_j^-(s',\cdot)$ is $L(s')$-Lipschitz. For noiseless targets and any reference $\nu$,
\begin{equation}
 \|y_{\mu_1}-y_\nu\|_{L_2(\D)}
 \le \gamma\!\left(\E_{s'}L(s')^2\|\mu_1(s')-\nu(s')\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:perturb}
\end{equation}
If $L(s')\le\bar L$, the bound is $\bar L\gamma\sqrt{dD_{\rm critic}}$. Conditional on $\mu_1$, later actors do not enter this one-step bound.
\end{proposition}
With common target-smoothing noise, action clipping is nonexpansive and the same comparison applies. This is BAR's finite-step decoupling statement: conditioned on the first actor, later refinements and final-policy displacement are absent from the one-step target bound. The bound controls target shift rather than asserting critic convergence.

\paragraph{Local flow interpretation and implementation scope.}
For an interior action, fixed critic, exact statewise solve, and $\beta=dh/C$, the projected-linearized step is $\Delta a_{\rm lin}=\beta g$, while the ideal proximal stationary equation is $\Delta a_{\rm prox}=\beta\nabla Q(a_0+\Delta a_{\rm prox})$. If the Hessian is locally Lipschitz and $\beta\|H\|<1$,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta a_{\rm prox}=\beta g+\beta^2Hg+O(\beta^3).
 \label{eq:local}
\end{equation}
Thus the ideal proximal and projected-linearized hops are first-order discretizations of the same local action flow. BAR instantiates their objectives with persistent ReLU actors and one Adam step; because later actors are independently initialized, this learned finite-step operator need not approach the identity as $h\to0$. The expansion explains the ideal update geometry, while the complete sweep evaluates the live actor--critic procedure.

For completeness, if an \emph{ideal} smooth map satisfies $\Psi_h(x)=x+h f(x)+h^2p(x)+O(h^3)$, then for fixed $K$ and $T\to0$,
\begin{align}
\Psi_{T/K}^{K}(x_0)
={}&x_0+Tf_0\\
&+T^2\left[\frac{K-1}{2K}J_0f_0+\frac{1}{K}p_0\right]+O(T^3).
\label{eq:composition}
\end{align}
Explicit Euler has $p_0=0$ and exact backward Euler has $p_0=J_0f_0$, yielding ideal leading flow bias proportional to $1/K$. In the fixed-depth small-$T$ regime, this gives refinement depth a formal role; the large-budget behavior of the persistent actor chain is established empirically below. The expansion remains conditional on the near-identity premise and is not uniform as $K\to\infty$.

\section{Experiments}
\paragraph{Protocol.}
We use the nine public D4RL-v2 locomotion datasets formed by HalfCheetah, Hopper, and Walker2d with \texttt{medium}, \texttt{medium-replay}, and \texttt{expert} data \citep{fu2020d4rl}. They combine three dynamics with three standardized data-collection regimes and a common normalized-return protocol, making them a controlled test of stability across dataset quality and coverage. The exact IDs are listed in the supplement. We sweep
$T\in\{.05,.1,.2,.4,.7,1.5,2.5,4,7,10,12,14,17,20\}$,
where $T=\tau=\alpha/2$ and $K=1$ is the canonical TD3+BC coefficient mapping. We test TD3+BC ($K=1$), proximal $K\in\{2,3,4\}$, and linearized $K\in\{2,3\}$ for $10^6$ critic updates. The proximal grid uses seeds 0--3; seeds 0--1 and 2--3 were executed on separate hosts and are reported as disjoint replication pairs. The linearized grid uses seeds 0--1. Intermediate evaluation cadence varied by run family; every reported sweep score uses the common ten-episode normalized mean at the final $10^6$-update checkpoint. Each $K$ is a randomized training procedure whose seed average estimates marginal performance; common initialization across depths is a variance-reduction design, not a validity condition. All conditions share the reported dataset, critic architecture, and number of critic updates, while a depth-$K$ run performs $K$ actor optimizer calls per delayed update.

\subsection{Refinement Depth Expands the Tested Envelope}
Table~\ref{tab:main} and Figure~\ref{fig:stability} show strict depth ordering in the four-seed proximal aggregate that weights each of the nine task means over the seven $T\ge4$ values equally: the mean rises $25.38\to41.20\to51.92\to63.97$, while collapsed cells fall $34\to22\to16\to8$ as $K$ increases from one to four. Relative to TD3+BC, $K=4$ gains $38.59$ points on average over tasks; the descriptive task-resampling 95\% interval is $[21.39,54.63]$, and eight of nine task means improve. Relative to $K=3$, the mean gain is $12.06$ with interval $[3.84,21.11]$ and seven of nine task means improve. The cellwise median gain is $.34$, while eight collapsed $K=3$ cells cross above the threshold at $K=4$ and none move in the opposite direction. Those eight cells contribute $54.7\%$ of the total $K=4$ minus $K=3$ gain summed over all 63 high-budget cells. This mean--median contrast localizes the added-depth benefit to lower-tail rescue, the effect targeted by the stability envelope. The raw-run collapse-risk difference is $-.119$ with a task-resampling interval of $[-.218,-.024]$, so both the continuous-score and thresholded-risk summaries support the expansion on this grid.

The projected-linearized realization reproduces the depth direction: $K=2\to3$ raises the high-budget mean from $36.68$ to $43.91$ and reduces collapsed cells from 29 to 24. This cross-realization agreement supports refinement depth as a design axis, while the stronger proximal results show that the update realization also matters. At $T=20$, raw linearized targets reach the action box in roughly 94--95\% of audited states and effective coefficients are heavily attenuated, placing the method in the finite-step, amortized, critic-coupled regime targeted by the stability experiments.

\subsection{Movement and Critic-Failure Audits}
We distinguish three empirical quantities. On a current-state validation batch,
$\widehat D_{\rm critic}=\E\|\mu_1(s_i)-a_i\|^2/d$ and
$\widehat D_{\rm final}=\E\|\mu_K(s_i)-a_i\|^2/d$ use online actors. On bootstrapped next-state pairs,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat D_{\rm target}=\E_i\|\mu_1^-(s_i')-a_{i+1}\|_2^2/d
 \label{eq:dtarget}
\end{equation}
uses the Polyak target actor; a smoothed version averages common TD3 noise draws. Equation~\eqref{eq:dtarget} is a sample-anchored next-state target-action displacement, not a direct policy-to-policy Wasserstein distance. It and $\widehat D_{\rm final}$ also use different states, anchors, and Polyak status.

For TD3+BC, P2, and P3 at $T\in\{4,7,10,14,20\}$, the audit covers 270 method checkpoints, or 90 matched environment--budget--seed cells per method. The current-state first-branch proxy correlates $.9999$ with deterministic Eq.~\eqref{eq:dtarget}. Three-hop proximal refinement has lower target-action displacement than TD3+BC in $88/90$ matched environment--budget--seed cells, with median ratio $.658$; the 18-cluster interval for the lower fraction is $[.944,1]$. Against $K=2$, it is lower in $84/90$ cells with median ratio $.903$. Together, these comparisons reveal BAR's intended asymmetric movement profile: refinement nearly always reduces critic-facing target displacement, yet it does not produce a typical-cell contraction of the final-policy proxy. The latter is lower than TD3+BC in only $36/90$ cells and has median ratio $1.057$; several large contractions make its arithmetic mean difference negative. Because the two proxies use different states, anchors, and Polyak status, this is an asymmetry in cross-method response rather than a direct within-run distance ratio. For the audited $K=3$ procedure, reduced exposure is a robust mechanism signature, although it is not sufficient by itself for return improvement: among the 84 cases with lower target displacement than $K=2$, $K=3$ loses by more than one score point in 17.

Collapsed runs exhibit orders-of-magnitude larger TD-error tails, critic magnitudes, and critic losses (Figure~\ref{fig:failure}). Under a frozen TD3+BC $T=1$ critic matched by environment and seed, median run-total actor-chain gain changes from $+2.11$ to $-1.42$ for stable versus collapsed linearized runs, and from $+2.51$ to $-1.06$ for proximal runs. This sign reversal identifies sharp cross-critic disagreement at failure. In a simulator screening, the median of checkpoint-level signed-error medians, $\widehat Q_1(s,a_K)-Q_{\rm MC}^{\rho_1}(s,a_K)$, is $-41.4$ over 55 stable checkpoints and $1.70\times10^{12}$ over five collapsed checkpoints. Together with the movement audit, these results connect BAR's exposure asymmetry to the critic-failure mode that dominates the high-budget phase diagram; the ranking subset is sparse and remains exploratory.

\subsection{Targeted Controls and Limitations}
Two actor-detached shadow critics share initialization, minibatches, target noise, and a fixed actor trajectory, isolating backup route for the measured critic-alignment outcome. Against the common first-route continuation, final-route error is larger in all eight four-checkpoint run averages under the primary state-median aggregation, although the direction holds in only five of eight final-checkpoint comparisons. This intervention shows that routing changes critic alignment along the training trajectory in the four post-hoc selected transition cells. Because neither shadow critic drives the actor, it does not estimate a routing effect on return or each route critic's own-continuation calibration.

In the same targeted eight-run set, sequential $K=3$, fixed-reference $K=3$, and a direct-final compute-matched control score $71.4$, $70.0$, and $59.4$ on average, with collapse counts $2/8$, $2/8$, and $3/8$. The near-match between sequential and fixed-reference refinement shows that re-centering alone is not necessary for rescue in this sample, while the direct-final gap favors a routed multi-actor procedure over added actor calls alone. Slowing only the one-hop actor-target Polyak rate by three gives $7/8$ collapses versus $6/8$ under the standard rate and rules out target lag as a sufficient explanation. These targeted controls narrow the mechanism without fully decomposing the complete depth intervention.

The proximal cross-depth grid uses four seeds, executed as two host-separated pairs; the linearized grid and mechanism audits use two. Exact per-run manifests for the newer proximal pair were not retained in the repository, so its same-procedure status rests on the reported launch configuration plus the archived score matrices rather than a complete configuration hash. The tasks span nine datasets but only three dynamics families, the collapse threshold is descriptive, and mechanism controls target post-hoc transition cells. Increasing $K$ changes local coefficient, persistent actor count, optimizer calls, re-centering, normalization trajectory, and routing together; the sweep therefore identifies the depth-indexed procedure rather than any component alone. We do not provide an offline rule for choosing $T$ or $K$. Actor-update time and actor memory scale as $O(K)$, while critic updates and dataset sample count are fixed. The ideal fixed-critic analysis motivates the live persistent one-Adam-step chain but does not certify it.

\section{Conclusion}
\method\ makes refinement depth a practical control over offline actor--critic stability. By allocating a nominal finite-step coefficient budget across persistent actors, it routes only the first actor into Bellman targets while using a separately refined final actor for deployment, structurally separating which branch determines exposure and reach. Across four proximal seeds, the equally task-and-budget-weighted $T\ge4$ mean is strictly ordered from $25.38$ at $K=1$ to $63.97$ at $K=4$, and collapsed cells fall from $34/63$ to $8/63$; both disjoint host-separated seed pairs reproduce the aggregate ordering. The finite-step perturbation bound and 270-checkpoint $K=3$ audit identify bootstrap-exposure decoupling as the mechanism signature accompanying this expansion, with most of the added-depth aggregate gain coming from low-score rescue. These results establish the end-to-end value of $K$ as BAR's refinement-depth variable. The ideal Wasserstein analysis explains its local geometry, while component-level causal decomposition remains open.
