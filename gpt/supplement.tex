\section{Proofs and Scope}
\subsection{Behavior anchoring and Wasserstein interpretation}
For a behavior distribution with conditional mean $b(s)=\E[a\mid s]$,
\[
\E[\|\mu(s)-a\|^2\mid s]=\|\mu(s)-b(s)\|^2+\E[\|a-b(s)\|^2\mid s].
\]
After averaging over states and dividing by the action dimension $d$, the second term is independent of $\mu$. For deterministic policies,
\[
W_2^2(\delta_{\mu(s)},\delta_{\nu(s)})=\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2,
\]
so the population squared behavior anchor is a rescaled integrated squared Wasserstein distance to the conditional-mean behavior map. This does not imply distance to the support of a multimodal behavior policy.

\subsection{Inexact learned-critic margin}
For hop $k\ge2$, suppose the predecessor $\mu_{k-1}$ is feasible and the realized actor satisfies
\[
\mathcal L_{h,k}(\mu_k\mid\mu_{k-1})\le \mathcal L_{h,k}(\mu_{k-1}\mid\mu_{k-1})+\varepsilon_k.
\]
With
\[
\mathcal L_{h,k}(\mu\mid\nu)=-\frac{2h}{C_k}\E_s\widehat Q(s,\mu(s))+\mathsf d_d^2(\mu,\nu),
\]
rearrangement gives
\[
\frac{2h}{C_k}\E_s[\widehat Q(s,\mu_k)-\widehat Q(s,\mu_{k-1})]-\mathsf d_d^2(\mu_k,\mu_{k-1})\ge-\varepsilon_k.
\]
If $\widehat Q=Q^\dagger+e$, then
\[
m_k^{Q^\dagger}=m_k^{\widehat Q}-\frac{2h}{C_k}\E_s[e(s,\mu_k)-e(s,\mu_{k-1})].
\]
Hence learned-objective descent transfers only if critic error is controlled along the realized path. No environment-return guarantee follows. The first sample-anchored hop lacks a literal deterministic-policy comparator unless one uses the population behavior map or an explicit behavior actor.

\subsection{First-hop target perturbation}
Let $F_{s'}(a)=\min_jQ_j^-(s',a)$. If each $Q_j^-(s',\cdot)$ is $L(s')$-Lipschitz, then
\[
|F_{s'}(a)-F_{s'}(b)|\le L(s')\|a-b\|_2.
\]
For $y_{\mu}=r+\gamma F_{s'}(\mu(s'))$,
\[
|y_{\mu_1}-y_{\mu_0}|\le\gamma L(s')\|\mu_1(s')-\mu_0(s')\|_2.
\]
Squaring and averaging proves the main-text bound. Under common TD3 target-smoothing noise, action clipping is nonexpansive and the same comparison holds. This bound does not include critic calibration error and does not imply stable fitted-Q iteration.

\subsection{Local explicit/implicit scope}
For a fixed smooth critic and inactive action constraints, write $g=\nabla_a\widehat Q(a_0)$, $H=\nabla_a^2\widehat Q(a_0)$, and $\beta=dh/C$. The action-metric linearized update is $\Delta a_{\rm lin}=\beta g$. An exact proximal stationary point satisfies
\[
\Delta a_{\rm prox}=\beta\nabla_a\widehat Q(a_0+\Delta a_{\rm prox})
=\beta g+\beta H\Delta a_{\rm prox}+O(\beta\|\Delta a_{\rm prox}\|^2),
\]
so for sufficiently small $\beta$,
\[
\Delta a_{\rm prox}=\beta g+\beta^2Hg+O(\beta^3).
\]
This is a fixed-critic local statement. The live chain uses persistent independently initialized actors and one Adam step per hop, so later actors need not approach the identity as $h\to0$.

For the actual ReLU critics, the action gradient is piecewise constant inside an activation region. The final locked residence audit uses six Hopper/Walker tasks, seeds 0--1, and 512 fresh anchors per run, for 6144 anchors total. The verified pooled full-horizon residence probabilities are
\[
0.954,\quad0.911,\quad0.842,\quad0.748
\]
at $T=.025,.05,.1,.2$. These values delimit the region in which a local affine interpretation can apply; they are not a learned convergence-order result.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
All headline score grids use the nine D4RL locomotion task variants and ten evaluation episodes at the final $10^6$-critic-update checkpoint. The proximal and projected-linearized headline grids use seeds 0--3. The proximal seed pairs 0--1 and 2--3 were executed on separate hosts.

The actor and critic are 256--256 ReLU MLPs; the actor has tanh output. We use batch size 256, Adam learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, discount $.99$, policy delay 2, target noise $.2$, target-noise clip $.5$ times the action bound, Polyak rate $.005$, and dataset state normalization by mean and standard deviation with $10^{-3}$ safeguard. A $K$-actor condition makes $K$ actor optimizer calls per delayed update.

The high-budget estimand uses $T\ge4$. Within each task, we average seeds and the seven high-budget values, then weight the nine task variants equally. Task-resampling intervals resample these nine task means with replacement for 100,000 draws. Because the nine variants share only three dynamics families, these intervals should be interpreted as fixed-benchmark descriptive uncertainty rather than population-level offline-RL confidence intervals.

\section{P0: BAR-P4 versus Direct Two-Actor Separation}
The locked P0 grid contains nine tasks, $T\in\{4,7,10,14,20\}$, seeds 0--1, and two procedures, for 180 completed final evaluations and 90 matched pairs. The direct two-actor procedure uses a target actor with coefficient $T/4$ and a deployed actor with coefficient $T$; both are directly data-anchored and only the target actor enters Bellman backups.

The primary statistic is BAR deployment minus two-actor deployment. The score-level merged archive gives task-equal mean $-2.3076$, paired median $+0.6811$, 55 BAR wins, one tie, and 34 two-actor wins. The 100,000-draw task-resampling interval is $[-7.2476,2.4118]$. \section{P1: Target-Value and Same-State Geometry Audit}
A verified 270-checkpoint audit covers TD3+BC, BAR-P3, and BAR-P4 over nine tasks, $T\in\{4,7,10,14,20\}$, and seeds 0--1. On a frozen next-state batch with common clipped target-smoothing noise, it computes
\[
\Delta_y^{\rm RMS}=\gamma\sqrt{\E\left[\left(\min_jQ_j^-(s',\mu_1^-(s')+\epsilon)-\min_jQ_j^-(s',a_{\rm next}+\epsilon)\right)^2\right]}.
\]
The same batch is used for deterministic target-branch displacement and final-actor displacement. The audit retains all finite values, including very large tails in critic-failure cells.

The main qualitative conclusion is that action displacement and target-value perturbation are related but not interchangeable. In stable BAR-P3 and BAR-P4 cells, the correlation between final/target displacement ratio and within-run $\Delta_y^{\rm RMS}$ is positive (approximately $.735$ and $.707$ respectively), while collapsed cells exhibit different, often reversed structure. This supports treating the value metric as a critic-sensitive diagnostic rather than a monotone performance certificate.

The audit also reconstructs a post-hoc feasible-comparator residual for re-centered hops using frozen final-checkpoint actor/optimizer states. This tests whether the conditional learned-objective comparison is locally plausible on the audit batch; it is not a global optimality gap and does not reconstruct the historical training minibatch.

\section{Failure Diagnostics}
Across the matched high-budget diagnostic panel, collapse is defined only from external normalized return. Collapsed proximal and linearized runs have much larger TD-error p99 values, larger critic magnitudes, and larger realized actor movement. Under a frozen TD3+BC common critic, median run-total actor gain is positive for stable runs and negative for collapsed runs. A separate seed-2/3 sensitivity on four environments preserves this sign split.

A targeted simulator-reference screening compares critic predictions with discounted Monte Carlo continuations from cloned MuJoCo states. Across 55 stable and five collapsed checkpoints, the median checkpoint-level signed critic errors are approximately $-41.4$ and $+1.70\times10^{12}$. The ranking subset is sparse, so this is calibration evidence rather than a general policy-ranking theorem.

\section{Additional Controls}
A smaller eight-run compute-matched study compares sequential K3, fixed-reference K3, and direct-final refinement. Their means are 71.4, 70.0, and 59.4. Slowing the K1 actor-target Polyak rate by $3\times$ does not rescue the selected failures. These post-hoc controls rule out target lag alone and suggest that added actor calls alone are insufficient, but they do not isolate a sweep-wide causal component.

Actor-detached route-shadow critics were also trained on eight selected runs. Native-continuation calibration favors the first route, but the common-continuation comparison fails its predeclared exact sign-flip criterion. Neither shadow changes actor training, so these results estimate calibration rather than return effects.

\section{Compute and Reproducibility Notes}
On one H200/JAX 0.10.2 stack, the compiled actor-phase timing/memory profiler gives 1.435 ms and 9.68 MB at $K=1$, versus 1.887 ms and 18.05 MB at $K=4$. These numbers include the profiler's scoped backend measurements and are not whole-training wall-clock or system-memory costs.

Historical provenance is uneven. The four-seed headline score matrices and launch configuration are archived, but exact per-run manifests for proximal seeds 2--3 were not retained. The P3 two-actor control's exact historical Git revision was never persisted; local source-file digests are retained and the missing revision is explicitly unrecoverable. New P0--P3 audits use frozen manifests, source hashes, dataset/normalization hashes, and independent verifiers where stated.
