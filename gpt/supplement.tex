\section{Wasserstein Minimizing-Movement Origin and Scope}
\subsection{Behavior anchoring as a deterministic Wasserstein movement cost}
For a behavior distribution with conditional mean $b(s)=\E[a\mid s]$,
\[
\E[\|\mu(s)-a\|^2\mid s]=\|\mu(s)-b(s)\|^2+\E[\|a-b(s)\|^2\mid s].
\]
After averaging over states and dividing by the action dimension $d$, the second term is independent of $\mu$. For deterministic policies,
\[
W_2^2(\delta_{\mu(s)},\delta_{\nu(s)})=\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2,
\]
so the population squared behavior anchor is a rescaled integrated squared Wasserstein distance to the conditional-mean behavior map. This identity is the geometric origin of BAR's $T/K$ construction. It does not imply distance to the support of a multimodal behavior policy.

\subsection{Ideal JKO/minimizing-movement composition}
Fix a normalized critic $\bar Q$ and define
\[
\mathcal P_h^{\bar Q}(\nu)\in\argmin_{\mu\in\mathcal M}
\left\{-\E_s\bar Q(s,\mu(s))+\frac{1}{2h}\mathsf d_d^2(\mu,\nu)\right\}.
\]
The normalized TD3+BC actor objective is this variational problem up to positive scaling, with nominal step $h=\alpha/2$. If a total nominal improvement time $T$ is split into $K$ equal steps, the ideal fixed-critic composition is
\[
\mu_K^{\rm ideal}=\left(\mathcal P_{T/K}^{\bar Q}\right)^K(\mu_0).
\]
Under the usual consistency and local regularity assumptions for minimizing movements, this composition approaches the metric gradient flow over total time $T$ as $K\to\infty$ with $T$ fixed. BAR was motivated by this time-splitting picture.

The live algorithm is not this exact JKO scheme. Each hop is represented by an independently initialized persistent neural actor, only one Adam step is taken per delayed update, the scale $C_k$ is recomputed, the critic moves across training iterations, and action projection/saturation can become active. In particular, for a later persistent actor the anchoring gradient need not vanish at $h=0$ when the actor differs from its predecessor. Thus the ideal composition motivates the construction but does not certify the learned chain.

\subsection{Local explicit/implicit expansion}
For a fixed smooth critic and inactive action constraints, write $g=\nabla_a\widehat Q(a_0)$, $H=\nabla_a^2\widehat Q(a_0)$, and $\beta=dh/C$. The action-metric linearized update is
\[
\Delta a_{\rm lin}=\beta g.
\]
An exact proximal stationary point satisfies
\[
\Delta a_{\rm prox}=\beta\nabla_a\widehat Q(a_0+\Delta a_{\rm prox})
=\beta g+\beta H\Delta a_{\rm prox}+O(\beta\|\Delta a_{\rm prox}\|^2),
\]
so for sufficiently small $\beta$ and locally Lipschitz Hessian,
\[
\Delta a_{\rm prox}=\beta g+\beta^2Hg+O(\beta^3).
\]
Hence the ideal explicit and implicit maps share the same first-order vector field. More generally, for a consistent one-step map
\[
\Psi_h(x)=x+hF(x)+h^2P(x)+O(h^3),
\]
$K$ compositions with $h=T/K$ have a fixed-$T$ global first-order bias of order $T^2/K$ relative to the exact flow, under the corresponding smooth fixed-operator assumptions. This is a local numerical statement only; it is not an explanation of the large-$T$ BAR phase diagram.

For the actual ReLU critics, the action gradient is piecewise constant inside an activation region. The final locked residence audit uses six Hopper/Walker tasks, seeds 0--1, and 512 fresh anchors per run, for 6144 anchors total. The verified pooled full-horizon residence probabilities are
\[
0.954,\quad0.911,\quad0.842,\quad0.748
\]
at $T=.025,.05,.1,.2$. These values delimit where a local affine interpretation is empirically plausible; they are not a learned convergence-order result.

\subsection{The three regimes are interpretive, not sharp phase boundaries}
The main text separates three regimes to organize the evidence:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Local gradient-flow regime:} small nominal and realized steps, where the fixed-critic Wasserstein/JKO interpretation is locally meaningful.
\item \textbf{Finite-step refinement regime:} the live neural chain departs from exact minimizing movements, but $T/K$ splitting still creates distinct target-facing and deployment branches.
\item \textbf{Nonlocal stability regime:} projection, saturation, critic drift, fitting error, normalization, and optimizer history dominate, so depth is treated as a complete stability intervention.
\end{enumerate}
These are not mathematically identified thresholds in $T$. In particular, the residence values at $T\le.2$ provide local-scope evidence but do not define a universal cutoff separating the regimes across tasks or seeds.

\section{Finite-Step Statements}
\subsection{Inexact learned-critic margin}
For hop $k\ge2$, suppose the predecessor $\mu_{k-1}$ is feasible and the realized actor satisfies
\[
\mathcal L_{h,k}(\mu_k\mid\mu_{k-1})\le \mathcal L_{h,k}(\mu_{k-1}\mid\mu_{k-1})+\varepsilon_k.
\]
With
\[
\mathcal L_{h,k}(\mu\mid\nu)=-\frac{2h}{C_k}\E_s\widehat Q(s,\mu(s))+\mathsf d_d^2(\mu,\nu),
\]
rearrangement gives
\[
\frac{2h}{C_k}\E_s[\widehat Q(s,\mu_k)-\widehat Q(s,\mu_{k-1})]-\mathsf d_d^2(\mu_k,\mu_{k-1})\ge-\varepsilon_k.
\]
If $\widehat Q=Q^\dagger+e$, then
\[
m_k^{Q^\dagger}=m_k^{\widehat Q}-\frac{2h}{C_k}\E_s[e(s,\mu_k)-e(s,\mu_{k-1})].
\]
Hence learned-objective descent transfers only if critic error is controlled along the realized path. No environment-return guarantee follows. The first sample-anchored hop lacks a literal deterministic-policy comparator unless one uses the population behavior map or an explicit behavior actor.

\subsection{First-hop target perturbation}
Let $F_{s'}(a)=\min_jQ_j^-(s',a)$. If each $Q_j^-(s',\cdot)$ is $L(s')$-Lipschitz, then
\[
|F_{s'}(a)-F_{s'}(b)|\le L(s')\|a-b\|_2.
\]
For $y_{\mu}=r+\gamma F_{s'}(\mu(s'))$,
\[
|y_{\mu_1}-y_{\mu_0}|\le\gamma L(s')\|\mu_1(s')-\mu_0(s')\|_2.
\]
Squaring and averaging proves the main-text bound. Under common TD3 target-smoothing noise, action clipping is nonexpansive and the same comparison holds. This bound does not include critic calibration error and does not imply stable fitted-Q iteration.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
All headline score grids use the nine D4RL locomotion task variants and ten evaluation episodes at the final $10^6$-critic-update checkpoint. The proximal and projected-linearized headline grids use seeds 0--3. The proximal seed pairs 0--1 and 2--3 were executed on separate hosts.

The actor and critic are 256--256 ReLU MLPs; the actor has tanh output. We use batch size 256, Adam learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, discount $.99$, policy delay 2, target noise $.2$, target-noise clip $.5$ times the action bound, Polyak rate $.005$, and dataset state normalization by mean and standard deviation with $10^{-3}$ safeguard. A $K$-actor condition makes $K$ actor optimizer calls per delayed update.

The high-budget estimand uses $T\ge4$. Within each task, we average seeds and the seven high-budget values, then weight the nine task variants equally. Task-resampling intervals resample these nine task means with replacement for 100,000 draws. Because the nine variants share only three dynamics families, these intervals should be interpreted as fixed-benchmark descriptive uncertainty rather than population-level offline-RL confidence intervals.

\section{P0: BAR-P4 versus Direct Two-Actor Separation}
The locked P0 grid contains nine tasks, $T\in\{4,7,10,14,20\}$, seeds 0--1, and two procedures, for 180 completed final evaluations and 90 matched pairs. The direct two-actor procedure uses a target actor with coefficient $T/4$ and a deployed actor with coefficient $T$; both are directly data-anchored and only the target actor enters Bellman backups.

The primary statistic is BAR deployment minus two-actor deployment. The score-level merged archive gives task-equal mean $-2.3076$, paired median $+0.6811$, 55 BAR wins, one tie, and 34 two-actor wins. The 100,000-draw task-resampling interval is $[-7.2476,2.4118]$. The predeclared three-point decision rule labels this \emph{unresolved}.

\section{P1: Target-Value and Same-State Geometry Audit}
A verified 270-checkpoint audit covers TD3+BC, BAR-P3, and BAR-P4 over nine tasks, $T\in\{4,7,10,14,20\}$, and seeds 0--1. On a frozen next-state batch with common clipped target-smoothing noise, it computes
\[
\Delta_y^{\rm RMS}=\gamma\sqrt{\E\left[\left(\min_jQ_j^-(s',\mu_1^-(s')+\epsilon)-\min_jQ_j^-(s',a_{\rm next}+\epsilon)\right)^2\right]}.
\]
