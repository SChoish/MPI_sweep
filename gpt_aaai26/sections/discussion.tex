\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The evidence supports BAR as an empirical fixed-nominal-budget refinement bundle. It does not identify which constituent--smaller per-actor coefficient, additional actor optimization, persistent capacity, re-centering, normalization, or target/deployment routing--causes the gain. A targeted compute-matched study finds similar means for sequential and fixed-reference three-actor chains, so re-centering alone is not established. Slower target averaging does not reproduce the selected rescues, but that intervention was also small and did not audit realized displacement.

The ideal proximal results are not an algorithmic guarantee for live BAR. One Adam update need not solve the subproblem or satisfy the conditional comparator premise; persistent actors may differ even when $h=0$, violating the near-identity condition behind the local composition expansion. The frozen-critic audit checks ideal action-space explicit--implicit consistency, not the optimizer-driven actor chain.

The main grid uses two seeds and nine datasets from only three dynamics families. Dataset-cluster intervals quantify variation over this finite suite, not population uncertainty over all offline-RL tasks. Collapse is an operational threshold, no offline selector for $T$ or $K$ is supplied, and diagnostic screens were chosen after observing frontier behavior. Finally, the study is limited to TD3+BC; earlier multi-algorithm experiments are not used as central evidence because their complete configurations and selection metadata are not part of this audited package.
