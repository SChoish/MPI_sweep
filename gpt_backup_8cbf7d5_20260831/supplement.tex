\section{Implementation Semantics}
\subsection{Budget Mapping and Empirical Expectations}
The trainer parameter is $T=\tau=\alpha/2$ in the canonical scale-normalized TD3+BC objective. At $K=1$, the critic coefficient is therefore $2T/C_1$, exactly matching the baseline mapping $\alpha=2T$. At depth $K$, each hop uses $h=T/K$ and coefficient $2h/C_k$, so the nominal integration time remains $T$ while actor calls and persistent actor state grow with $K$. Thus $T$ is a nominal finite-step coefficient budget, not compute, wall-clock time, or a realized action-distance constraint. Unless another distribution is written explicitly, actor-loss expectations are empirical averages over the current dataset-state minibatch; the Q-scale safeguard is $\epsilon=10^{-6}$.

\subsection{Actor and Critic Update Order}
At every critic update, the implementation samples one minibatch, performs one clipped-double-Q critic update, and, every second critic update, executes the actor chain. The proximal path first updates $\mu_1$ toward sampled dataset actions, then Polyak-updates the target copy of $\mu_1$ and the target critic, and finally updates persistent actors $\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_K$ in sequence toward stop-gradient predecessor actions. The later actors see the already-updated online predecessor on the same minibatch. Only the target copy of $\mu_1$ supplies actions to Bellman targets; $\mu_K$ is evaluated.

The linearized path uses the same critic, minibatch, actor persistence, routing, and update order. It constructs a projected action-space target for each hop and takes one mean-squared-error Adam update. For the first hop its reference is the sampled dataset action; later references are updated predecessor actors.

\subsection{Normalization Factors}
The implementation computes a stop-gradient Q scale at different reference actions depending on the realization and hop. Let $t$ index a delayed policy update, ``pre'' denote the actor before its current Adam step, and ``$+$'' the actor after that step. The proximal factors are
\begin{align}
C_{1,t}^{\mathrm P}
&=\sg\!\left(\E_s\left|\widehat Q_t(s,\mu_{1,t}^{\mathrm{pre}}(s))\right|+\epsilon\right),\label{eq:s-cp1}\\
C_{k,t}^{\mathrm P}
&=\sg\!\left(\E_s\left|\widehat Q_t(s,\sg\mu_{k-1,t}^{+}(s))\right|+\epsilon\right),\quad k\ge2.\label{eq:s-cpk}
\end{align}
The linearized factors are
\begin{align}
C_{1,t}^{\mathrm L}
&=\sg\!\left(\E_{(s,a)\sim\D}|\widehat Q_t(s,a)|+\epsilon\right),\label{eq:s-cl1}\\
C_{k,t}^{\mathrm L}
&=C_{k,t}^{\mathrm P},\quad k\ge2.\label{eq:s-clk}
\end{align}
Thus the projected linearization matches the per-coordinate action metric and nominal coefficient but not the first-hop Q-normalization point. We avoid the stronger phrase ``fully scale-matched.''

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Implementation-level proximal actor chain}
\label{alg:s-prox}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE actors $\mu_{1:K}$ with separate Adam states, critic $\widehat Q$, minibatch $B$, $h=T/K$
\STATE Evaluate $C_1^{\mathrm P}$ at the pre-update output of $\mu_1$
\STATE Take one Adam step on $-(2h/C_1^{\mathrm P})\E_B\widehat Q(s,\mu_1(s))+\E_B\|\mu_1(s)-a\|^2/d$
\STATE Polyak-update target actor $\mu_1^-$ and target critic $Q^-$
\FOR{$k=2,\ldots,K$}
  \STATE Set $\nu_k=\sg\mu_{k-1}^{+}(s)$ and evaluate $C_k^{\mathrm P}$ at $\nu_k$
  \STATE Take one Adam step on $-(2h/C_k^{\mathrm P})\E_B\widehat Q(s,\mu_k(s))+\E_B\|\mu_k(s)-\nu_k\|^2/d$
\ENDFOR
\STATE Use $\mu_1^-$ in critic targets and $\mu_K$ for evaluation
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Why the Ideal Map Does Not Certify the Live Chain}
The ideal local analysis supposes a one-step map $\Psi_h$ with $\Psi_0=\mathrm{Id}$ and a consistent expansion in $h$. The live chain does not satisfy this automatically. Because later actors are independently initialized and remain separate persistent networks, at $h=0$ their objective is still
\begin{equation}
\E_s\|\mu_k(s)-\sg\mu_{k-1}(s)\|_2^2/d.
\end{equation}
If the two actors differ, one Adam step has a nonzero anchoring gradient. Therefore the live parameter update need not be near-identity as $h\to0$. The frozen-critic audit in Section~\ref{sec:frozen} compares ideal action-level explicit and implicit solutions; it does not test this neural optimizer map. The proximal analysis is consequently an ideal local motivation, not an algorithm-level guarantee.

\section{Proofs}
\subsection{Behavior-Anchor Decomposition}
Let $b(s)=\E[a\mid s]$. Conditional bias--variance decomposition gives
\begin{equation}
\E[\|\mu(s)-a\|_2^2\mid s]
=\|\mu(s)-b(s)\|_2^2+\E[\|a-b(s)\|_2^2\mid s].
\end{equation}
Averaging over $s$ and dividing by action dimension $d$ shows that the population squared behavior anchor equals the integrated squared distance from $b$ plus an additive conditional-action-variance term independent of $\mu$. Ratios of sample-anchored quantities do not cancel this floor; paired differences do at the population level.

For a state distribution $\rho$, define $\mathsf d_{d,\rho}^2(\mu,\nu)=\E_{s\sim\rho}\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2/d$ and write $\mathsf d_d$ when $\rho$ is clear. For deterministic state-conditioned policies $\pi_\mu(\cdot\mid s)=\delta_{\mu(s)}$ and $\pi_\nu(\cdot\mid s)=\delta_{\nu(s)}$,
\begin{equation}
W_2^2(\pi_\mu(\cdot\mid s),\pi_\nu(\cdot\mid s))=\|\mu(s)-\nu(s)\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Hence the per-coordinate integrated action metric is a fixed rescaling of squared Wasserstein--2 distance in the Dirac-policy limit.

\subsection{Conditional Learned-Critic Margin}
For a fixed scale $C_k>0$, reference $\nu_k$, and nominal step $h_k$, define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{h_k,k}(\mu\mid\nu_k)
=-\frac{2h_k}{C_k}\E_s\widehat Q(s,\mu(s))
+\mathsf d_d^2(\mu,\nu_k).
\end{equation}
Suppose $\nu_k$ is feasible and
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{h_k,k}(\mu_k\mid\nu_k)
\le \mathcal L_{h_k,k}(\nu_k\mid\nu_k)+\varepsilon_k.
\end{equation}
Substituting both losses and rearranging yields
\begin{equation}
\frac{2h_k}{C_k}\E_s[\widehat Q(s,\mu_k)-\widehat Q(s,\nu_k)]
-\mathsf d_d^2(\mu_k,\nu_k)\ge-\varepsilon_k.
\end{equation}
This is a comparator inequality conditional on the realized objective value, not an unconditional property of one Adam step. A sampled action at a state is not generally a feasible deterministic policy, so the first-hop literal comparator must be the conditional-mean behavior map or a learned behavior actor.

If $\widehat Q=Q^\dagger+e$, then
\begin{align}
m_k^{Q^\dagger}=m_k^{\widehat Q}
-\frac{2h_k}{C_k}\E_s[e(s,\mu_k)-e(s,\nu_k)].
\end{align}
If $|e|\le\delta_k$ on both realized actions, this gives
$m_k^{Q^\dagger}\ge-\varepsilon_k-4h_k\delta_k/C_k$. A true-return theorem would additionally require occupancy-control and value-estimation assumptions that are not made here.

\subsection{First-Hop Target-Perturbation Bound}
Let $F_{s'}(a)=\min_j Q_j^-(s',a)$. If each $Q_j^-(s',\cdot)$ is $L(s')$-Lipschitz, then the pointwise minimum is also $L(s')$-Lipschitz:
\begin{align}
|F_{s'}(a)-F_{s'}(b)|
&\le \max_j|Q_j^-(s',a)-Q_j^-(s',b)|\\
&\le L(s')\|a-b\|_2.
\end{align}
For noiseless TD targets,
\begin{equation}
|y_{\mu_1}-y_\nu|\le\gamma L(s')\|\mu_1(s')-\nu(s')\|_2.
\end{equation}
Squaring and averaging proves the main-paper bound. In a same-noise comparison, adding common target noise preserves the action difference before clipping; projection onto the action box is nonexpansive. The bound concerns one-step target-value perturbation and does not by itself establish critic convergence or return improvement.

\subsection{Local Explicit--Implicit Expansion}
Fix a critic and an interior action $a_0$. Set $g=\nabla_a\widehat Q(a_0)$, $H=\nabla_a^2\widehat Q(a_0)$, and $\beta=dh/C$. The metric-matched explicit step is $\Delta a_{\mathrm L}=\beta g$. An ideal exact proximal stationary point satisfies
\begin{equation}
\Delta a_{\mathrm P}=\beta\nabla_a\widehat Q(a_0+\Delta a_{\mathrm P}).
\end{equation}
Assume the Hessian is locally Lipschitz and $\beta\|H\|<1$. Taylor expansion gives
\begin{equation}
\nabla_a\widehat Q(a_0+\Delta a)=g+H\Delta a+O(\|\Delta a\|^2),
\end{equation}
and local boundedness implies $\Delta a_{\mathrm P}=O(\beta)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
(I-\beta H)\Delta a_{\mathrm P}=\beta g+O(\beta^3),
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\Delta a_{\mathrm P}=\beta g+\beta^2Hg+O(\beta^3).
\end{equation}
If one assumes only $C^2$ regularity, the safe remainder is $o(\beta^2)$ rather than $O(\beta^3)$. A ReLU critic is piecewise smooth, so the displayed expansion is an ideal local statement away from activation boundaries.

\subsection{Fixed-Depth Composition}
\begin{proposition}
Let a smooth ideal one-step map satisfy $\Psi_h(x)=x+h f(x)+h^2p(x)+O(h^3)$. For fixed $K$ and $T\to0$,
\begin{align}
\Psi_{T/K}^{K}(x_0)
={}&x_0+Tf_0\\
&+T^2\left[\frac{K-1}{2K}J_0f_0+\frac{1}{K}p_0\right]+O(T^3).
\end{align}
where $f_0=f(x_0)$ and $J_0=Df(x_0)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $K$, induction gives $x_j=x_0+jhf_0+O(h^2)$. Smoothness implies $f(x_j)=f_0+jhJ_0f_0+O(h^2)$ and $p(x_j)=p_0+O(h)$. Summing $K$ increments and substituting $h=T/K$ gives the result. Coordinate explicit Euler has $p_0=0$; exact backward Euler has $p_0=J_0f_0$. Both are first order for fixed $K$, with leading ideal flow bias proportional to $1/K$. This is not a uniform $K\to\infty$ result and does not describe the live persistent-actor chain without the near-identity premise.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Grid, Estimands, and Complexity}
The primary grid uses
\begin{equation}
T\in\{.05,.1,.2,.4,.7,1.5,2.5,4,7,10,12,14,17,20\},
\end{equation}
with seeds 0--3 for TD3+BC ($K=1$) and proximal $K=2,3,4$, and seeds 0--1 for linearized $K=2,3$. Each proximal procedure therefore contains $9\times14\times4=504$ runs, and each linearized procedure contains 252. The proximal seeds form two host-separated execution pairs, 0--1 and 2--3, which permit a direct replication check of the full aggregate depth ordering. All score matrices are archived; exact per-run configuration manifests for the newer pair are not retained. The cross-$K$ estimand is the expected performance of each complete randomized procedure. Identical critic initializations across $K$ are not required for unbiased marginal seed averages; using common random numbers would be a variance-reduction design, not a validity condition. Paired win counts are interpreted under the actual seed coupling and are descriptive.

The primary high-budget estimand uses the upper seven values of the ordered fourteen-point grid, $T\ge4$. Within each task it averages the available seeds and those seven budgets, then weights the nine task means equally. This is an operational stress-region aggregate, not a claim of pointwise monotonicity in either $T$ or task. A seed-mean environment--budget cell is called collapsed when its normalized score is below 20; the complete per-budget table and thresholds 10, 20, 30, and 40 expose sensitivity to both aggregation choices.

Actor-update time and actor parameter/optimizer memory are $O(K)$. The number of critic updates and the fixed-dataset sample source are held constant. The method is not compute matched across depth in the complete sweep. A targeted control holds three actor optimizer calls fixed.

\subsection{Task-Cluster Uncertainty}
We resample the nine task-level high-budget means with replacement for 100,000 draws using fixed analysis seed 20260830 and report descriptive percentile intervals. The unit is a D4RL task, not an environment--budget cell. For P4 minus TD3+BC, the four-seed mean difference is $38.59$ with 95\% interval $[21.39,54.63]$; eight of nine task means are positive. For P4 minus P3, the difference is $12.06$ with interval $[3.84,21.11]$, and seven of nine task means are positive. These intervals do not remove the dependence among the three datasets sharing one dynamics family, and nine tasks are not treated as a random sample from all continuous-control problems.

For raw seeded high-budget runs, P4 minus TD3+BC changes collapse risk by $-.349$, with task-resampling interval $[-.524,-.175]$. P4 minus P3 changes it by $-.119$, with interval $[-.218,-.024]$. Both thresholded-risk intervals exclude zero on the four-seed proximal grid, consistent with the continuous-score rescue. The eight seed-mean cells rescued from below 20 contribute $54.7\%$ of the total P4-minus-P3 gain summed over all 63 high-budget cells.

\section{Compact Diagnostics Re-Audit}
The compact audit pack was rebuilt from existing local NPZ/per-state inputs; it did not rerun policy training or simulator rollouts. It verifies 270 target-exposure rows, 180 failure-diagnostic rows, 60 simulator checkpoints with 720 states, and eight route runs with 32 arm-pair checks. Across 2,135 checksummed source files (3.18 GB), primary-key duplication, paired-state mismatches, NaNs, and infinities were all zero. No manuscript aggregate table was used as an input to deterministic claim reproduction.

\subsection{Target-Action Exposure}
The mechanism audit covers $T\in\{4,7,10,14,20\}$, all nine environments, two seeds, and three methods (TD3+BC, P2, and P3), yielding 270 method checkpoints and 90 matched cells per method. It directly tests the exposure signature for P3; it does not measure P4 target movement. The target metric is

\subsection{Simulator Aggregation}
Simulator estimates average four common-random-number rollouts at each state and then forms state errors. There are 12 states per checkpoint. The values $-38.7$ and $1.70\times10^{12}$ in the earlier analysis are pooled-state medians. The checkpoint-level aggregation used in the main text is
\begin{align}
\operatorname{median}_{\rm ckpt}\!\left(
\operatorname{median}_{\rm state}
[\widehat Q_1(s,a_K)-Q_{\rm MC}^{\rho_1}(s,a_K)]
\right),
\end{align}

\section{Frozen-Critic Local Audit}
\label{sec:frozen}
The frozen-critic study uses the final TD3+BC $T=1$ critic for all nine environments and two seeds. At 512 interior dataset actions per run, it compares the projected explicit step and a projected fixed-point solution of the ideal implicit action equation for $h\in\{.001,.003,.01,.03,.1\}$. Fifteen of 18 environment--seed runs contain at least one converged, projection-free eligible point. Every eligible run has maximum relative explicit--implicit discrepancy below $2.7\%$; the median run maximum is $.55\%$. Three runs have no eligible point. This supports local action-level agreement but does not test a universal $O(h^2)$ rate or the persistent neural actor optimizer.

\section{Artifact and Reproducibility Notes}
The compact audit pack publishes run/checkpoint summaries and deterministic claim reproduction while leaving multi-gigabyte state arrays local. It verifies source counts, keys, state pairings, common target-noise files, normalization hashes, and finite values. For anonymous release, local absolute paths in checkpoint fields, the audit manifest, and checksum list must be replaced by logical artifact identifiers before submission. The released compact bundle does not copy those local paths.

The release includes primary training and sweep code, compact CSVs, figure generation, and verification scripts. Exact dependency lower bounds are available, but a fully locked historical environment and complete hardware inventory are not. Some legacy targeted-control launch wrappers are documented rather than fully reconstructed. These points motivate the ``Partial'' answers in the main-paper reproducibility checklist.

\section{Interpretation Ledger}
The evidence supports the following statements:
\begin{itemize}
\item On the shared high-budget grid of nominal $T$ values, the tested proximal procedures have a substantially broader aggregate stability envelope as refinement depth increases.
\item The complete aggregate K1-to-P4 depth ordering reproduces in both disjoint host-separated seed pairs.
\item P4 improves both aggregate continuous score and thresholded collapse risk over P3 on the four-seed grid; rescued cells contribute $54.7\%$ of the summed gain.
\item BAR structurally separates critic-facing and deployed actors, and P3 empirically realizes the intended asymmetry by usually reducing target-action displacement without typical final-proxy contraction.
\item Collapsed runs exhibit critic divergence signatures and negative gain under a controlled common critic.
\end{itemize}
The evidence does not support the following stronger statements:
\begin{itemize}
\item one Adam step solves the ideal proximal subproblem;
\item the local Wasserstein expansion certifies the live algorithm;
\item target routing, re-centering, or actor compute alone causes the sweep-wide improvement;
\item lower target displacement alone determines return;
\item final-route critics are universally worse calibrated to their own continuations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproducibility Checklist}
The main PDF includes the official AAAI-26 checklist with all 31 responses. The non-\texttt{yes} answers correspond to concrete artifact boundaries rather than unstated exclusions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Theory empirical support---partial.} The finite-step routing bound is exact under its stated Lipschitz condition, while the Wasserstein analysis concerns ideal fixed-critic maps. The frozen-critic audit supports their local action geometry but does not certify the persistent neural one-Adam-step chain.
\item \textbf{Claim-elimination code---no; code completeness/release---partial.} The primary trainer, sweep analysis, diagnostics, figures, and verifier are included and intended for MIT-licensed release, but some exact historical targeted-control launch wrappers are unavailable.
\item \textbf{Search log, final parameters, comments, seeds, and infrastructure---partial.} The tested $T/K$ grid and retained configurations are explicit, but there is no comprehensive development-search log, exact K4/newer-pair run manifest, line-by-line paper cross-reference, full environment lockfile, or complete historical hardware inventory.
\item \textbf{Statistical significance---partial.} We report equal-task aggregates, raw counts, threshold sensitivity, and 100,000-draw task-/cluster-resampling intervals with fixed analysis seeds. These are descriptive tests on nine tasks from three dynamics families, without multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory inference.
\end{itemize}
